Meeting Video and Key Frames

Meeting Video and Key Frames

Section titled “Meeting Video and Key Frames”

When a meeting’s video is recorded, LMA also keeps key frames: a picture of the shared screen each time it changes to something new and holds still — each slide of a deck, each page of a document, each new view of an application. Key frames are stored on the meeting beside the transcript, with times on the transcript’s clock, so they can be lined up with what was said while each one was on screen. The meeting page shows them as a strip of thumbnails, below.

On the meeting page

Section titled “On the meeting page”

A meeting with key frames has a Screen share tab on its page, with the number of frames in its label: a strip of thumbnails in time order, each labelled with the time it went up. The tab appears once the first frames have loaded, including during a live meeting when the first frame is taken. Frames of the meeting’s camera video rather than shared content are not shown or counted (see Camera frames), and frames that repeat the screen before them are hidden and not counted until asked for (see Repeated frames).

  • Selecting a thumbnail shows the picture large and moves the transcript to that time, highlighting the segment that was under way, for when the Transcript tab is next opened; Show in transcript opens it there. During a live meeting this pauses following the newest segment, as scrolling up does; Resume in the transcript returns to it.
  • After the meeting, selecting a thumbnail also moves the meeting page’s player to that frame, without playing it. Play recording from here opens the Transcript tab and plays the recording from the frame selected, and Open full size opens the picture in a new tab.
  • A slide named in the summary is a thumbnail on the Summary tab; selecting it opens this tab with that frame selected.
  • During the meeting, new frames are added to the end of the strip as they are taken, and the strip follows them unless it has been scrolled back.
  • Long meetings load a hundred frames at a time; the next hundred load as the strip is scrolled to its end (or with Load more).
  • Find text on the shared screen filters the strip to the frames whose slide text holds every word typed, in any order, ignoring case and accents, with the line that matched under each thumbnail. It searches the whole meeting: the frames not loaded yet are loaded first, and the frames shown are read again if any of them was loaded before its text was read (the text follows its frame by a few seconds). A selected frame shows its text under the picture.
  • Labels: a frame is labelled with its own title, a few words read with its slide text; a frame without one that begins one of the meeting’s chapters is labelled with the chapter’s title.
  • Repeated frames: when some frames were found to show the same screen as an earlier one, the strip says how many are hidden, and Show all N frames shows them in place (Hide repeated frames hides them again). A search looks through the frames the strip shows, so with repeats hidden it finds the first frame of each screen. A repeated frame selected from the summary is shown either way.
  • Export slides (PDF) opens every frame, without the repeated ones, as a printable deck; see Exporting the slides.

The pictures are shown through links signed by the server for whoever opened the page, after the same access check as the transcript, and they last an hour; the strip renews them when one has expired. A meeting without key frames shows no section at all.

Slide text

Section titled “Slide text”

Each key frame’s text is read once, a few seconds after the frame is stored: a vision model (Key frame text in the Amazon Bedrock Models section of the Configuration page, Amazon Nova Lite unless changed) transcribes the readable text, top to bottom, and adds a line in brackets for a chart, diagram or picture, for example [Bar chart of revenue by quarter]. It is told not to describe or name people, and to leave out the meeting’s video tiles, small terminal windows and status strips, the operating system’s menu bar and taskbar, and window title bars. The model’s answer begins with two lines: what the frame shows (CONTENT: SCREEN, CAMERA or MIXED; see Camera frames) and a title of at most eight words naming the main shared content. An answer without them is read as SCREEN with no title, all of it text. The text is stored on the frame (SlideText; empty when the frame holds none), with ContentKind and Title, and is what:

  • the meeting assistant reads first, before it looks at any picture;
  • the meeting page searches;
  • the meeting-history knowledge base indexes, so the meeting history tool and the MCP search_meetings tool find a meeting by what was shown as well as by what was said. The text is added to the meeting’s transcript document, as a SHARED SCREEN section with each frame’s time, when the meeting’s summary is written (stacks that summarize with Bedrock, which write that document).

A vision model was chosen over Amazon Textract by measurement, on the synthetic meeting’s frames with their true text known:

A model reading a small, dense strip of text can fall into a loop, writing the same few words until it reaches its output budget (measured on a Desktop Capture App meeting: three of 24 frames). The budget is 1,200 tokens, about twice a dense page, and what is kept collapses a run of words or lines repeated in a row to one copy and drops text that is still mostly repetition after that. An answer cut off at the budget is logged and keeps its text, without its last, cut-off line.

Camera frames

Section titled “Camera frames”

A key frame is taken whenever the picture changes and then holds still, so a camera view that fills the screen (a meeting window showing a room camera, in the Desktop Capture App’s capture of the whole display) yields frames when the people in it move. On one measured meeting 10 of 24 frames were of that kind. The vision model therefore also says what each frame shows: SCREEN (shared slides, documents or applications), CAMERA (the meeting’s video tiles or camera), or MIXED (both, with shared content worth reading). A CAMERA frame is stored with no text and no title, and is left out of the screen share summary, the slide changes the chapters are placed by, the meeting’s knowledge-base document, the assistant’s view, the Screen share strip and the exported deck. Its images are kept like any other frame’s. Frames read before ContentKind was stored, or not yet read, have none and are treated as shared content. How well the model tells the kinds apart depends on the model.

Repeated frames

Section titled “Repeated frames”

A shared application whose content keeps updating, or a page scrolled a little, can give several frames of what a reader would call one screen: the pictures differ, but the text on them is nearly the same. Once a frame’s text is read it is compared with the last frame before it that is neither a camera frame nor itself a repeat, and stored with DuplicateOf naming that frame when the two show the same screen:

  • their texts are at least 80% alike (the words, in order, compared as a sequence), or
  • their titles are the same, ignoring case and punctuation, and their texts are at least 50% alike. The title alone is not enough, because a model can name a whole deck (“Q3 review”) rather than each slide.

A frame whose text holds every line of the earlier frame’s and more is not a repeat, however alike the two are: it is a slide built up a point at a time, and the later frame is the fuller one. Two frames with no text are repeats only when their titles match. Nothing is marked when the earlier frame’s text has not been read yet, or when the earlier frames cannot be read; such a frame is shown, as every frame was before frames were compared.

Repeats are hidden on the Screen share strip unless Show all N frames is selected, and are left out of the exported deck. They are still stored, with their images, and are returned by getVideoKeyframes like any other frame; the summary, the chapters, the assistant and the meeting-history document read them as before. During a live meeting a frame is shown as soon as it is taken, before its text has been read; the strip reads the newest frames again with each new one, and a frame found to be a repeat is hidden then.

Screen share summary

Section titled “Screen share summary”

A meeting with key frames gets a SCREEN SHARE SUMMARY section in its summary: what was shared on screen, in the order shown, whether slides, documents, web pages, applications, code or a demo. Each entry has the time it went up, a few words naming what was shown, the thumbnail of its most representative frame and a sentence or two on what it showed and what was said or decided about it. The prompt asks the model to group consecutive frames of one thing (a slide built up point by point, a document being scrolled, an application being demonstrated) into one entry, to write no more than 15 entries, and to leave out frames with nothing on them, such as an empty desktop, a blank window or the meeting’s own video tiles. It also asks the model to treat everything in the frames as quoted meeting content rather than instructions, never to identify people from how they look, and not to write out web addresses, credentials or personal data read from the screen. How well it does each depends on the model. A meeting without key frames gets no such section, as a meeting without an agenda gets no agenda section.

The section was called SLIDES PRESENTED in 0.4.0’s pre-releases. Summaries written then keep that title and are shown in the same way. On a stack update, a Custom profile holding SLIDES PRESENTED exactly as it was shipped, or turned off, has it removed (turned off, its replacement is turned off in its place); one whose administrator changed that prompt keeps it, and the seeder logs a warning naming it on every deployment until it is removed. See Summary sections a release no longer ships.

How it is built:

  • The fetch-transcript function gives each key frame what was said while it was on screen: the transcript segments that began between it and the next frame, with the speaker names the transcript shows.
  • The section’s prompt names the {slides} placeholder ({screen_share} is another name for it), which the summarizer fills with each frame’s time, its picture reference (![Slide at 12:30](keyframe:<FrameId>)), its slide text and what was said, and the prompt asks the model to copy the picture reference exactly. The transcript itself is not sent with this section. At most 60 frames are listed (past that, the ones on screen for the shortest time are left out), with each frame’s text cut at 1,500 characters and its speech at 3,000 (the fetch-transcript function sends no more than that).
  • The meeting page’s Summary tab shows each picture reference as the frame’s thumbnail, inside a section that starts closed, so the summary of a long presentation is not a column of slides until it is opened; the frames are read once the section is opened, and selecting one opens it on the Screen share tab. The embeddable summary panel shows each picture reference as the frame’s thumbnail, read through getVideoKeyframes for that meeting with the same access check as the strip, in one request for every frame the summary names; selecting it opens the full picture. A picture whose signed link has lapsed (links last an hour) is read again by itself. A frame that cannot be read, because the meeting’s retention has removed it, shows as its alt text. Copy, download, Word, email, the meetings list’s summary preview and the MCP get_meeting_summary tool show the alt text too, and so does the summary document for the meeting-history knowledge base.
  • A key frame of the meeting is the only picture a summary shows, in this section or any other. Any other image a summary names, by any address, is shown as its alt text and is not fetched.

The section is a prompt in the summary_prompt_templates section like any other, so it can be edited, replaced or removed (set it to NONE) on the Transcript Summary section of Configuration ▸ Configuration. A prompt of your own can use {slides} or {screen_share} as well.

The measurements below were made with the SLIDES PRESENTED prompt, which asked for one entry per slide and had no limit on the number of entries. They have not been repeated with the SCREEN SHARE SUMMARY prompt, so its entry counts and costs are not yet measured; the input is the same, and the output should be no longer.

Measured on the synthetic meeting (14 key frames: ten slides, one of them taken twice, and three pages of a document), every model copied every picture reference exactly, and none copied the Image: label with it:

Chapters

Section titled “Chapters”

The summary has a CHAPTERS section: one line per main topic of the meeting, - [MM:SS] Title, at the time the topic was first discussed or a new slide started it. The meeting page reads that section and shows a Chapters list beside the summary on the Summary tab, in place of the section itself. Selecting a chapter opens the Transcript tab at its time and, after the meeting, plays the recording from there. The chapters are also marks on the seek bar of the page’s player. In the Screen share strip, the frame that went up within 30 seconds of a chapter’s start is labelled with the chapter, unless the frame has a title of its own; a chapter that began without a change of slide labels no frame, and a camera frame is never matched.

How it is built:

  • The fetch-transcript function returns, when asked (IncludeTimes), the transcript with each line’s time and a line at each change of the shared screen with the start of the new frame’s text: [04:00] (The shared screen changed: Q3 Budget - Spend is 4% under plan).
  • The section’s prompt names the {timed_transcript} placeholder, which the summarizer fills with that text. The prompt asks for one line per chapter, with each time taken from a line of the transcript. Chapters therefore work for any meeting with a transcript, with or without video; the slide changes only help place them.
  • Chapter times are on the transcript’s clock. To play the video recording from a chapter, the page converts the time to the recording’s clock using the key frame nearest before it, as each frame carries both. A Virtual Participant’s video starts before transcription and falls further ahead at each reconnection of its transcription, which the frames follow. A Virtual Participant meeting whose video has no key frames plays the audio recording by default for this reason. The audio recording starts with transcription, so it needs no conversion. See Lining the recording up with the transcript.
  • The page reads the section from a generated summary, or from under its CHAPTERS heading once the summary has been edited by hand. It accepts the usual variations of list mark, brackets and bold, and leaves out any line without a readable time.

Measured with the shipped prompt on a synthetic 24-minute meeting with five topics (two started by a new slide, three by discussion only), every model returned only lines the page reads and took every time from the transcript:

The prompt asks for the line where a topic is first discussed (not where an agenda mentions it) and for at least two minutes between chapters, because without those instructions the smaller models split the meeting into far more chapters or placed them at the agenda’s mention of each topic. One synthetic meeting is a small sample, so expect chapter boundaries from the smaller models to be less reliable on real meetings.

Exporting the slides

Section titled “Exporting the slides”

Export slides (PDF), on the Screen share tab, opens the meeting’s key frames in a new tab as a deck, one landscape page per frame in time order, each with its picture at full size (1568 pixels wide), its time, its label (as on the strip), and its slide text; camera frames and repeated frames are left out, whether or not the strip is showing all frames. It then opens the browser’s print dialog, where Save as PDF saves it. All of the meeting’s frames are read again for the export, so their links are fresh, and the dialog opens once the pictures have loaded. The tab opens as soon as the button is selected, and shows that the deck is being prepared until then.

The deck is printed by the browser rather than built as a PDF file in the page, so any language or script on the slides prints as it shows on screen. A PDF library embeds only the fonts it ships, which leaves characters outside them blank. The trade-off is that the user takes the last step in the print dialog. The browser must allow the page to open a new tab; if it blocks the tab, the section says so.

Asking the assistant about the shared screen

Section titled “Asking the assistant about the shared screen”

The meeting assistant has a get_meeting_frames tool that reads the key frames a question is about — the last few, those in a time range, or the one on screen at a moment — each with its slide text and what was said while it was on screen. For example:

  • “Summarize that slide.” / “What’s on this slide?”
  • “Translate the last slide into French.”
  • “Show me the last two slides.”
  • “What was on screen at 12:30?” / “Which slides did we see between 10:00 and 20:00?”

The text usually answers the question, so pictures are sent only when it does not: when the assistant asks to look (a chart, a diagram, a layout), or for a frame whose text has not been read yet. They go to the model as images when it reads them, which every model the stack offers does except Amazon Nova Micro; with Nova Micro the assistant answers from the text, the times and the transcript. At most four pictures are sent with one tool call, and at most ten frames are listed. The assistant is told to describe what a frame shows and never to identify people from their faces.

Where the answer appears decides how frames are shown:

The answer refers to a frame by its id and the page fetches a signed link for it through getVideoKeyframes, with the same access check as the strip.
The meeting chat (LMA ask, the Virtual Participant)Described in words. Everyone in the meeting reads the answer, and the frames show what was shared with them.

Which meetings have key frames

Section titled “Which meetings have key frames”
SourceKey frames
Virtual ParticipantYes, when Record meeting video is on for the Virtual Participant (the toggle in the Create Virtual Participant modal; the deployment default is the EnableVideoRecording stack parameter). The VP samples its own meeting display, the same picture its screen recording captures.
Desktop Capture AppYes, when Also record screen video is on in the app and the deployment records video (EnableVideoRecording). The WebSocket transcriber samples the video the app streams, as it arrives; see Desktop video.
Stream Audio, Browser Extension, Upload AudioNo. These sources carry no video.

Sampling follows the meeting’s recording: in the Virtual Participant it runs only while the meeting is being transcribed, so nothing is captured before the VP is admitted or while the meeting is paused from the chat; and nothing at all is captured from either source when video recording is off.

How a key frame is chosen

Section titled “How a key frame is chosen”

Once a second the VP takes a small grayscale copy of its display — a grid of 64 × 36 cells, each the average brightness of a block of the screen — and compares it with two earlier pictures:

  • The previous second’s, and the first picture of the still spell, to tell whether the picture is still moving. A slide transition, scrolling, a video or a camera is not captured while it moves; a frame is kept only once the picture has held still for two seconds (three without a screen-share signal, below). Comparing with the start of the spell as well means a picture that creeps a little every second, as a live web page can, does not count as holding still.
  • The last key frame’s, to tell whether the settled picture is new. Going back to the slide that was just captured, or a share that stops and starts again on the same slide, does not produce another frame.

A cell counts as changed when its brightness differs by more than 12 (of 255). Cells that keep changing — a strip of cameras beside the share, a video playing in one corner — are recognised as moving and left out of both comparisons, so they neither stop a slide from settling nor make it look new. No frame is kept while more than half the picture is moving (a quarter without a screen-share signal).

Each key frame is stamped with the moment its picture first appeared, not the moment it was confirmed, so a slide that went up at 12:30 is listed at 12:30, and never earlier than the frame before it was taken. No two frames are stamped less than a second apart, on the detector’s clock or on the transcript’s, and a meeting keeps at most 1,000.

How often frames are kept

Section titled “How often frames are kept”

A shared application whose content keeps updating, such as a live web page, a dashboard or a scrolling transcript, changes by about as much each time as one template slide differs from the next. On a measured 58-minute meeting with a browser showing such a page, the frames differed from the one before by 3–9%, and the four-second spacing alone let through 146 frames in 42 minutes, a median of 10 seconds apart. Raising the change threshold to separate the two would also drop slides (template slides differ by 4–7%, below), so the spacing adapts instead:

  • While the screen keeps changing, the second frame can follow the first after four seconds, the third waits 30 seconds after the second, and every later one 60 seconds after the one before.
  • Each 10 seconds that the screen is quiet — holding still, and no different from the last key frame — steps the wait back down one: from 60 to 30 seconds, then to four. A deck whose slides each stay up for longer than about 12 seconds (10 seconds quiet, plus the two seconds to settle) is therefore kept slide by slide; slides changed faster than that, on a screen that has been changing, can be skipped until the wait runs out.
  • A picture that differs by at least 10% from the one that had settled before it is a different screen rather than an update of the same one (a new application, a slide with another layout), and waits only the four seconds. It is judged against the picture just before it, not against the last key frame, because the small updates of a busy screen add up against an old frame.

Frames that still show the same screen after this are collapsed once their text is read; see Repeated frames.

Desktop video

Section titled “Desktop video”

The desktop capture app sends its screen or window as H.264 at 5 frames a second, with a full picture (an H.264 key frame) at least every two seconds. The transcriber decodes only those full pictures, as the video arrives, and judges each one exactly as the Virtual Participant judges its samples. The app gives no screen-share signal — what it captures is whatever the user chose to share with LMA — so every picture is judged against the stricter thresholds, except that two still pictures in a row (about four seconds) are enough to settle, close to the Virtual Participant’s three seconds. The spacing adapts in the same way (four seconds, then 30 and 60 while the picture keeps changing, stepping back down one for every 10 seconds it is quiet).

Each key frame’s time is placed on the transcript’s clock from the offset the app reports between the start of its audio and the start of its video, which is also how the recording is lined up with the audio. If the app restarts its video during the meeting, the frames after the restart are placed by the time they arrive and have no recording position, because the recording joins the two parts end to end.

What is stored

Section titled “What is stored”

Each key frame is two JPEG images in the recordings bucket, under lma-video-keyframes/<meeting>/ — a large one (1568 pixels wide) for reading the slide and a thumbnail (320 pixels wide) for lists — and an item on the meeting in the event-sourcing table (partition key kf#<CallId>). <meeting> is the CallId with every character other than letters, digits, _ and . replaced by _ and any leading and trailing _ removed, which is how meeting deletion finds the images. The item has:

FieldMeaning
FrameIdThe frame’s id: its time in milliseconds, zero-padded, and a short suffix. Frames sort by it in time order.
TimestampSeconds on the transcript’s timeline, the same clock as a transcript segment’s start time.
VideoOffsetSeconds into the meeting’s video recording, for playing the recording from that frame.
ImageUrl, ThumbnailUrlWhere the two images are stored.
SourceWhat took the frame: VIRTUAL_PARTICIPANT or DESKTOP_CAPTURE.
ShareDetectedtrue when the frame was taken while the platform showed a share; absent on platforms without the signal and for desktop video.
ChangeScoreThe part of the picture that differed from the previous key frame.
SlideTextThe text on the frame, read once after it is stored (see Slide text); empty when it holds none.
ContentKindWhat the frame shows, read with its text: SCREEN, CAMERA or MIXED (see Camera frames). Absent until read, and on frames read before it was stored.
TitleA few words naming the frame’s shared content, when the model gave them.
DuplicateOfThe FrameId of an earlier frame showing the same screen, set with the text when the frame is a repeat (see Repeated frames); absent otherwise.

Retention, access and deletion

Section titled “Retention, access and deletion”
  • Retention. Key frames are kept as long as the meeting’s turn-by-turn transcript: the items carry the transcript’s expiry, and a lifecycle rule on lma-video-keyframes/ deletes the images after TranscriptionExpirationInDays days. A shorter transcript retention set on the Configuration page (Retention) applies to the items and not to the images, which the bucket rule keeps for the parameter’s number of days.
  • Access. Key frames are meeting data with the transcript’s access rule: getVideoKeyframes and onAddVideoKeyframe return them to the meeting’s owner, to the people it is shared with, and to administrators. Sharing or unsharing a meeting shares or unshares its key frames with it. The images are private objects in the recordings bucket, which the web UI’s identity cannot read: getVideoKeyframes checks the caller’s access to the meeting and then signs an hour-long link to each image, and only for an image stored in that meeting’s own folder of this deployment’s bucket.
  • Deletion. Deleting a meeting deletes its key frames and their images, and its video recording: the file named on the meeting record, and any Virtual Participant recording segments left under lma-video-chunks/.

Reading key frames

Section titled “Reading key frames”

The AppSync API has a query and a subscription:

The query returns frames in time order, up to 500 at a time (100 by default), with ThumbnailSignedUrl and ImageSignedUrl filled in; afterFrameId returns only the frames after the one given, which is how the meeting page fetches a frame the subscription announced. frameId returns just that frame, and with it limit, nextToken and afterFrameId are not used. frameIds returns the frames it names, up to 100, in the order named, each once, leaving out any that do not exist, also without limit, nextToken or afterFrameId; it is how the summary’s pictures, and a chat answer’s, are resolved, in one request rather than one per frame. Giving both frameId and frameIds is refused. The subscription carries no signed links. As with onAddTranscriptSegment, its selection must include Owner and SharedWith.

The ADD_VIDEO_KEYFRAME event

Section titled “The ADD_VIDEO_KEYFRAME event”

A key frame reaches the meeting as an ADD_VIDEO_KEYFRAME record on the call data stream, which the call event processor stores and publishes. A producer that uploads its own images can send the same event:

Timestamp, both URLs (HTTPS), a known Source and a FrameId of at most 64 characters are required; an event without them is logged and dropped, not retried. The meeting must already exist. Owner and sharing are taken from the meeting, and expiry from the deployment’s transcript retention, not from the event.

What key frames are not used for

Section titled “What key frames are not used for”

Key frames are pictures of the shared screen, kept so the meeting’s content can be found and read again. LMA does not analyse the people who appear in them: no face detection or facial recognition is run on key frames or on the meeting video, and nothing identifies participants from video. Speaker names come from the meeting platform and the transcript, as described in Transcription & Translation. Where the platform gives no screen-share signal a key frame can show camera tiles, as described above; they are stored like any other key frame and not analysed.
