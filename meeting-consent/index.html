Meeting Consent

Meeting Consent

Section titled “Meeting Consent”

LMA can tell a meeting’s participants that the meeting is being recorded and transcribed, and can wait for them to agree before it records or transcribes anything. Whether it does either is the consent mode, set for the whole deployment on the Configuration page and, where the deployment allows it, chosen again for a single meeting when the meeting starts. What participants replied, who attested on their behalf, and any screenshots kept as evidence are stored on the meeting in a consent record, beside its transcript.

The record is supporting evidence of what was said in the meeting. It is not a legal determination that consent was given, and LMA does not decide which rules apply to a meeting: recording and transcription laws differ between countries, states and organisations, some require every party’s consent and some only one party’s, and some set requirements a chat reply does not meet. Choose the mode with whoever advises your organisation on those rules. Nothing on this page is legal advice.

Current state: the consent settings, the consent record and its API, the evidence check and the meeting page’s Consent section are in place. The Virtual Participant runs the consent flow: it posts the notice in the meeting chat, reads the replies typed there or said aloud, and withholds or stops recording and transcription accordingly (see Virtual Participant). Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App run it too: they show the notice, ask the user to confirm, and the WebSocket transcriber records that on the meeting and withholds or stops recording and transcription accordingly (see Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App). The browser extension does not run it yet; how the transcriber treats a client that shows no consent step is a setting. See Flows by capture method.

The three modes

Section titled “The three modes”
ModeWhat participants seeWhen the meeting is recorded and transcribed
OffNothingAlways. This is how LMA behaves when consent has never been set up, and the shipped default.
NotifyA notice that the meeting is recorded and transcribed, and how to objectFrom the start, until a participant objects. An objection stops recording and transcription, and is recorded.
Opt-inA request to agree, with the phrase to replyNot until the consent rule below is met. An objection from anybody stops it again. Nothing is recorded or kept before then. A Virtual Participant still sends the meeting audio to the transcription engine while it waits, only to hear replies said aloud, unless Read spoken replies is off; see Spoken replies.

An opt-in meeting is granted by one of two consent rules:

  • Each participant consents. Every participant listed as present has replied with consent. A participant who joins later and has not replied withholds recording and transcription again until they do.
  • The meeting owner attests that everyone agreed. The person who owns the meeting in LMA confirms that everyone agreed: out loud, in the chat, or in the invitation’s terms. An objection from any participant still stops recording and transcription, whatever the owner attested.

Status, and when a meeting may be captured

Section titled “Status, and when a meeting may be captured”

Each meeting’s consent record has a status, worked out again every time something is recorded on it:

StatusMeaning
not_requiredThe meeting runs in mode Off, or its consent was never started.
pendingNotify: the notice has not been recorded as posted yet. Opt-in: the rule is not met yet.
grantedNotify: the notice was posted and nobody has objected. Opt-in: the rule is met and nobody has objected.
objectedA participant’s latest reply is an objection.

The record also says whether the meeting may be recorded and transcribed now (CaptureAllowed), and this is the one value a capture client acts on:

  • Opt-in: only while the status is granted.
  • Notify: unless the status is objected.
  • Off: always.

A meeting with no consent record is judged by the deployment’s mode: in a deployment whose mode is opt-in it may not be captured until its consent has been started and granted, because the absence of a record is not consent.

Every change of status, or of whether the meeting may be captured, is added to the record’s status history with the time and a short reason (“1 participant objected”, “every participant consented”), so the record shows when recording stopped and resumed as well as why.

How a reply is read

Section titled “How a reply is read”

A participant’s reply — a chat message, or something said in the meeting — is read as consent or as an objection by the phrases in the Meeting Consent settings:

  • It is an objection when it contains one of the objection phrases as whole words, ignoring case, punctuation and spacing.
  • Otherwise it is consent when it contains one of the consent phrases.
  • Objections are looked for first, so “I do not consent” is an objection although it contains “consent”, and “I agree, but I object to the recording” is an objection.
  • Anything else is neither, and is not recorded.

Each participant’s latest reply is the one that counts: somebody who objected and then consented has consented, and the other way round. Replies are matched to participants by their display name in the meeting, compared without regard to case or extra spacing.

The Meeting Consent settings

Section titled “The Meeting Consent settings”

An Admin sets the deployment’s default in the Meeting Consent section of Configuration ▸ Configuration (see the Configuration Guide). A saved change applies to meetings whose consent starts after the save; a meeting already under way keeps the mode and rule it started with.

The configuration presets set these settings together with the related retention and privacy ones: Internal meetings – notify participants sets Notify, and Regulated – consent required, transcript not retained sets Opt-in with the each-participant rule, a stricter choice only per meeting, clients without a consent step refused and the Virtual Participant leaving after an objection. Under Regulated, Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App cannot collect each participant’s consent, so Start is refused from them (see Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App), and a Virtual Participant on the Webex join never meets the each-participant rule and cannot move to owner-attests, so its meeting is not transcribed (see What each platform supports). On the Teams browser join the rule is met only by consent said aloud. Nothing is recorded or kept before consent under Regulated; while it waits a Virtual Participant listens for spoken replies, so the meeting audio is processed by the transcription engine and only the reply events are kept, unless Read spoken replies is off. No preset sets Read spoken replies, so applying one leaves it as it is. Regulated – notify with opt-out, transcript not retained sets the same as Regulated except the mode, which is Notify: capture runs from the start and stops when a participant objects, and a meeting may use Notify or the stricter Opt-in, not Off. Notify and objections work on every Virtual Participant join, and Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App run their notify step and capture from Start, while clients without a consent step are still refused (see Clients without a consent step). Standard puts back the shipped defaults. Apply one from the editor’s Actions ▸ Apply a preset…, or choose one at deploy time with the ConfigurationPreset stack parameter; see Configuration presets.

In the two texts, {LMA_USER} is the person using LMA in the meeting, {meeting} the meeting’s name, and {consent_phrase} and {objection_phrase} the first phrase of each list. Any other text in braces is posted as written.

With Allow a different choice per meeting off, a meeting may still be started with a stricter choice than the default — opt-in in a notify deployment, or each participant in an owner-attests deployment — but not a less strict one, and not with its own notice text.

The settings are read when they are needed, with no cache, because they decide whether a meeting may be captured. If they cannot be read, starting a meeting’s consent fails rather than falling back to off, and so does reading a meeting that has no consent record; a meeting with a record is read without the settings, since its record decides its own gate.

On the meeting page

Section titled “On the meeting page”

The meeting page’s header names the consent setting that applied to the meeting and its status (Consent Opt-in · granted), or Consent enforcement off for a meeting whose consent was off or never started; selecting it opens the record. The Details tab’s Capture and processing section shows the same setting with the status. While a live meeting in notify or opt-in mode has recording and transcription withheld, a warning above the tabs says that transcription is paused until consent is recorded (or that a participant objected), from 30 seconds after the meeting started, with a link to the record.

A meeting whose consent was in notify or opt-in mode has a Consent section on its Details tab. It shows:

  • the mode and, for opt-in, the rule;
  • the status, and whether the meeting may be recorded and transcribed now (Allowed or Withheld);
  • the notice posted, and when it was first posted;
  • the participants: everyone listed as present, then anybody else who was listed or replied, each with their latest reply (Consented, Objected or No reply);
  • the owner’s attestation, when there is one;
  • the evidence images as thumbnails, each with the vision model’s verdict, confidence and summary when it has been checked; selecting one opens it full size;
  • a timeline of everything, oldest first: consent started, notices posted, replies and attestations, and each change of status with its reason. Replies are placed by when LMA recorded them, the order the status is decided in.

If the record cannot be read when the page opens, the section says so rather than disappearing, and the summary download has no record to include until one is read. If a later read fails, the section shows the error above the last record read, and the download carries that record.

The images are shown through links signed for whoever opened the page, after the same access check as the transcript, and last an hour. An image that has expired (see What is stored) or was deleted after its check is shown as such, with its entry and check. The section updates as the record changes, whoever changed it. A meeting whose consent was never started, or ran in mode off, shows no section.

Three actions, each offered only to the people the backend lets do it:

  • Record an objection — the owner, anyone the meeting is shared with, and Admins. Enter the participant’s display name and, optionally, what they said. It is recorded as source ui under your username, and stops recording and transcription in a notify or opt-in meeting until that participant consents.
  • Attest that everyone agreed — the meeting’s owner only, and only for an opt-in meeting under the The meeting owner attests rule, where it is what grants the meeting. Choose how everyone agreed (out loud, in the chat, or by accepting the invitation’s terms) and, optionally, add a statement. It grants the meeting unless somebody objected.
  • Check with model (or Check again) on an evidence image — the owner and Admins. See Checking evidence with a vision model.

In the summary download

Section titled “In the summary download”

The summary’s downloads and its copy to the clipboard, under Download in the meeting page’s header, add the consent record after the summary: the mode, rule and status, whether capture was allowed at the time of the download, the notice, the attestation, each participant’s latest reply, the timeline, and each evidence image’s check, followed by the note that the record is supporting evidence and not a legal determination. A meeting without a consent record downloads as before. The email link carries the summary only.

Flows by capture method

Section titled “Flows by capture method”

The consent record, its rules and its API are shared by every way a meeting reaches LMA. Each capture method decides how the notice is posted and how replies are read.

Capture methodNoticeRepliesStatus
Virtual ParticipantPosted in the meeting chat when the Virtual Participant is admitted, and again when somebody joinsRead from the meeting chat by display name, and from what is said, under the name of the participant shown speaking; an attestation or an objection made in the meeting page’s Consent section (or through the API); screenshots of the chat and the participant list kept as evidenceAvailable. See Virtual Participant for what each platform supports.
Stream AudioShown to the LMA user, with a copy button, to read out or post in their meetingAttested by the LMA user, who owns the meeting (opt-in is met by the rule The meeting owner attests); an objection is reported with Participant objectedAvailable. See Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App.
Desktop Capture App (macOS and Windows)As for Stream Audio, with a copy buttonAs for Stream Audio; evidence is a frame of the screen-video source selected in the appAvailable in builds that include it. Earlier builds show no consent step and are treated as clients without a consent step. See The Desktop Capture App.
Browser extensionAs for Stream AudioAs for Stream AudioNot available yet; treated as a client without a consent step.
Upload AudioNone: the recording already exists—Not applicable. A recording should only be uploaded with the consent its participants gave when it was made.

Until a capture method runs the flow, its meetings have no consent record. A streaming client’s are captured or refused as the setting Streaming clients without a consent step says. In a deployment whose mode is opt-in, getMeetingConsent reports a meeting with no record as not allowed.

A capture client follows the same steps whichever method it is:

  1. Create the meeting as it does today (the meeting record must exist), and in an opt-in meeting send no transcript or recording yet.
  2. Start consent with startMeetingConsent, naming the meeting’s mode and rule only when they differ from the deployment’s. The answer holds the notice text to post, its placeholders filled.
  3. Post the notice where participants will see it, and record that it was posted with recordMeetingConsentNotice.
  4. Keep the participant list current with setMeetingConsentParticipants as people join and leave, leaving out LMA’s own participant.
  5. Record each reply that reads as consent or an objection (see How a reply is read) with recordMeetingConsentEvent, and any attestation the owner makes with attestMeetingConsent.
  6. Gate capture on CaptureAllowed in every answer: start sending the transcript and recording when it becomes true, and stop when it becomes false.
  7. Keep evidence where it can: store a screenshot under the meeting’s evidence folder, record it with addMeetingConsentEvidence, and check it with verifyMeetingConsentEvidence.

Virtual Participant

Section titled “Virtual Participant”

The Virtual Participant (VP) runs the consent flow as a capture client, following the steps above. When the deployment’s mode is Off and the meeting chose nothing else, it does nothing new: it reads the mode before it joins, finds it off, and joins, records and transcribes exactly as it always has, without calling the consent API. The one exception is a VP that cannot read its own row before joining, where a per-meeting choice would be stored: it then withholds capture until a later read succeeds (see Choosing the mode for one meeting). In every other mode it posts the notice in the meeting chat once it is admitted, records that it did, keeps the participant list on the record current, reads replies typed in the chat and listens for replies said aloud (see Spoken replies), and acts on CaptureAllowed in every answer the API gives.

What “capture” means here is everything the VP takes from the meeting: the transcript, the audio recording, the meeting chat it adds to the transcript (see Meeting Chat in the Transcript), the voice assistant’s input, the key frames and the screen recording. The VP gates them with the same switch the LMA pause and LMA start chat commands use, so starting after consent is the same path as resuming after a pause.

Choosing the mode for one meeting

Section titled “Choosing the mode for one meeting”

The Create Virtual Participant form has a Meeting consent choice: the deployment’s setting (the default), Off, Notify, Opt-in with each participant consenting, or Opt-in with the meeting owner attesting. The each-participant choice is not offered for Webex, and for Teams it carries a note that on the Teams browser join participants consent aloud (see What each platform supports). Any choice other than the deployment’s is stored on the VP’s row (consentMode and consentRule in createVirtualParticipant) and read by the VP when it starts, so scheduled meetings keep it too. Whether the deployment allows the choice is decided as for any capture client: with Allow a different choice per meeting off, a less strict choice is not used. The VP applies that rule itself before joining, so it knows whether to withhold capture, and the resolver applies it again when the VP starts the meeting’s consent; when the resolver refuses the meeting’s choice, the VP runs the deployment’s mode instead. A meeting that chose Off in a deployment whose mode is not Off is captured as usual, and the VP still starts its consent, in mode off, once the meeting exists, so that the record shows consent was not required for it and the resolver’s check applies to it too. The meeting assistant’s MCP tools that start and schedule a VP do not offer the choice yet; a VP they start uses the deployment’s setting.

The VP reads the Meeting Consent section with no cache when it starts. If it cannot read it, and the meeting did not ask for opt-in, it does not assume Off: it withholds capture, as for opt-in, until the consent API tells it the meeting’s mode, and then behaves as that mode says. A Virtual Participant stack deployed on its own without its ConfigurationTableName parameter has no settings to read, so it behaves this way in every meeting; LMA’s main stack always passes the parameter. In the same way, if it cannot read the meeting’s own choice from its row (it tries three times before joining), it withholds capture and does not start the meeting’s consent until a later read succeeds, because the choice it could not read may have been opt-in. While the meeting runs a consent flow, LMA help also lists LMA consent and LMA no consent.

Notify

Section titled “Notify”

The VP records and transcribes from the start, as it does without consent, and posts the notice in the chat as soon as it is in the meeting. Once the notice is recorded as posted and nobody has objected, the status is granted, and the VP keeps screenshots as evidence (see Evidence screenshots). When somebody joins later, the notice is posted again, at most once a minute.

Opt-in

Section titled “Opt-in”

An opt-in meeting starts with capture withheld: the VP joins, but writes no transcript and no recording, does not start the voice assistant or the screen recording, and adds no chat messages to the transcript; messages sent while capture is withheld are not added later either. Unless Read spoken replies is off, it does send the meeting audio to the meeting’s transcription engine, on a session of its own, but only to hear consent replies and objections said aloud: everything else it hears is discarded inside the VP (see Spoken replies). Because nothing is transcribed, the VP sends the meeting’s START event itself, which creates the meeting record its consent needs, and then posts the consent request. The VP’s detail line on the Virtual Participants page reads Waiting for consent: … with what it is waiting for, and an LMA start typed in the chat is answered with “Live Meeting Assistant cannot start until consent is given.” When the record says the meeting may be captured, the VP posts that consent was given and the usual start message, starts transcription and recording, keeps evidence screenshots, and clears the detail line. If somebody typed LMA pause while the VP was waiting, it says that consent was given but stays paused until LMA start.

  • Each participant consents. Every participant listed as present must reply with consent, in the chat or aloud (see Replies in the chat and Spoken replies). Each consent is acknowledged with who is still awaited, for example “Thank you, Bo Chen. Still waiting for Cy Diaz and 1 unidentified participant.” When somebody joins while the meeting is being recorded, the record lists one more participant who has not consented, so recording and transcription pause until they do; the notice is posted again with a line saying so, and the detail line shows what is awaited. This is the record’s own rule: a participant who joins later and has not replied withholds capture again. Who counts as present is described in Who is present.
  • The meeting owner attests. The request is followed by “Recording starts when confirms in LMA that everyone has agreed.” The owner confirms with Attest that everyone agreed in the meeting page’s Consent section (see On the meeting page), or with attestMeetingConsent through the API. The VP reads the record every 10 seconds, so an attestation reaches it within about that long. Replies are still read and recorded as evidence, but no reply is taken as the owner’s attestation: the chat and the speaker indicator show a display name, which cannot be matched reliably to the LMA user who owns the meeting.

Objections

Section titled “Objections”

In every mode an objection, typed in the chat or said aloud, stops capture at once, before anything is written: the VP turns the transcript and recording off and stops the screen recording, then posts ” objected. Live Meeting Assistant has stopped recording and transcribing this meeting.”, records the objection (the sender’s or speaker’s display name, the words, chat or voice as the source, and the time) and keeps evidence screenshots. The detail line reads Not recording: a participant objected to recording. A spoken objection’s own words were said while the meeting was being captured, so they are the last line of its transcript. An objection said aloud and also typed in the chat, by the same participant or with either one unnamed, within 30 seconds and while capture is still stopped by the first, is acted on and recorded once.

  • With Virtual Participant leaves after an objection off (the default) the VP stays, and listens for spoken replies while capture is stopped. Each participant’s latest reply is the one that counts, so if the objector later replies with consent, in the chat or aloud, capture resumes, as the acknowledgement says. The transcript and the audio recording resume; the screen recording does not. It stops for the rest of the meeting the first time capture is withheld after it started, and what it recorded until then is kept on the meeting as usual.
  • With it on, the VP also posts its usual goodbye and leaves. Its status ends COMPLETED with “Left after objected to recording.” and the end trigger consent-objection.

An objection recorded somewhere else, by a signed-in user with Record an objection in the meeting page’s Consent section or through the API (recordMeetingConsentEvent), reaches the VP at its next read of the record. It stops capture in the same way and posts “An objection for was recorded in LMA. …”.

Replies in the chat

Section titled “Replies in the chat”

The VP reads a chat message as a reply only when the whole message is a reply, as it reads its chat commands: case, punctuation and spacing are ignored, and an LMA addressee may stand anywhere in it.

  • Consent is a message that is one of the consent phrases (“I consent”, “i agree!”, “LMA, I accept”), or LMA consent.
  • An objection is a message that is one of the objection phrases (“I object”, “Stop recording.”), LMA no consent or LMA object, or a message of up to 200 characters addressed to LMA that contains an objection phrase anywhere (“LMA, please stop recording now”).
  • Objections are looked for first, so “LMA I do not consent” is an objection.
  • Anything else is ordinary chat. In particular “I agree with Bo’s point” and “I don’t agree with the roadmap” are not replies, although they contain default phrases: a meeting chat is full of such sentences.

This is stricter than the reading in How a reply is read, which looks for a phrase anywhere in a reply; the VP does not use that reading for the chat or for speech. LMA consent, LMA no consent and LMA object work whatever phrases are configured, so they are the forms to suggest when a deployment’s phrases are long or in another language.

A consent reply needs a sender. One whose sender the platform does not show is not recorded, and under the each-participant rule it is answered with “Live Meeting Assistant could not tell who sent that reply, so it was not recorded as consent.”, followed on the Teams browser join, which shows no chat senders, by “Say “LMA, I consent” aloud instead.” (with the deployment’s first consent phrase), where spoken replies are read. An objection without a sender still stops capture, and is recorded for Unidentified participant. A sender whose display name reads as one of the list’s placeholders (Unidentified participant 1) is not counted as present and their consent is not recorded, so a name cannot fill the seat it reads as. A reply that a chat observer reports twice within five seconds is acted on once, and the VP never reads its own messages as replies.

Spoken replies

Section titled “Spoken replies”

With Read spoken replies on (the shipped default), the VP also reads replies said aloud, on every join and with either transcription engine, and every notice it posts is followed by “Replies may be typed in this chat, or said aloud starting with “LMA”: say “LMA, I consent” or “LMA, I object”.” (with the deployment’s first consent and objection phrases). With it off, the VP reads the chat only, as it did before spoken replies, sends no meeting audio anywhere while consent is awaited, does not read spoken objections from the transcription while capture runs, and follows every notice instead with “Live Meeting Assistant reads replies in this chat; an objection made out loud takes effect when records it in LMA.”, which Record an objection in the Consent section does. The VP reads the setting before it joins; if it cannot read the Meeting Consent section, it reads the chat only.

What counts as a spoken reply. Only an utterance addressed to LMA is a reply, because people say “I agree” and “I don’t agree” in conversation far more often than they type them as a whole message. Addressed, it is read as an addressed chat message is (see Replies in the chat), with the deployment’s phrases, the whole utterance as the speech engine finalises it being the reply:

  • Consent is LMA consent, or LMA with one of the consent phrases and nothing else (“LMA, I consent.”, “I agree, LMA”).
  • An objection is LMA object or LMA no consent, LMA with one of the objection phrases, or an utterance of up to 200 characters addressed to LMA that contains an objection phrase anywhere (“LMA, please stop recording now”). Objections are looked for first.
  • A bare phrase is not a reply when said aloud: “I consent.” and “Stop recording!” are ordinary speech, although the same words typed in the chat are replies.

Speech engines often write LMA as its letters, so “L M A” and “L.M.A.” are read as LMA. Only final results are read, never the engine’s partial results.

Who said it. A spoken reply is attributed to the participant the VP shows as speaking during it, the same name the transcript gives that audio: on Amazon Transcribe, the active speaker the platform showed for most of the utterance; on the MicroVM engine, the attendee the row is named after, without the engine’s voice label (spk_0), which is not a name. A named speaker also counts as seen for Who is present. A reply whose speaker the VP cannot name (nobody was shown speaking yet, or the platform shows a placeholder) is handled as a chat reply without a sender: a consent is not recorded, and under the each-participant rule the chat says “Live Meeting Assistant heard consent said aloud but could not tell who said it, so it was not recorded.”; an objection still stops capture and is recorded for Unidentified participant. Each reply is recorded with voice as its source. The VP’s own voice assistant is never read: it is not in the meeting audio the VP listens to, it has its own channel or session on both engines, and a reply attributed to one of the VP’s own names is ignored.

While capture runs (Notify, or Opt-in after consent), the VP reads the final participant results of the meeting’s ongoing transcription, and acts on a spoken objection exactly as on one in the chat.

While consent is awaited (Opt-in before consent, after an objection, or after somebody joins under the each-participant rule), nothing is captured, so the VP opens a separate session to the meeting’s transcription engine, Amazon Transcribe or the MicroVM engine, chosen as transcription chooses it, and streams the meeting audio to it from the moment it is admitted. With Amazon Transcribe this is its own streaming request, with the meeting’s language settings; with the MicroVM engine it is its own session, on the MicroVM the meeting’s transcription uses when one is running, or else on one launched for the meeting and then kept for its transcription once consent is given. If no MicroVM can be started, Amazon Transcribe is used, as for transcription. Every final result is attributed and read as above, and then discarded inside the VP:

  • what it heard is not written to Kinesis, so it never reaches the transcript, the summary or anything built from the transcript;
  • it is not written to the audio recording, which records silence while capture is withheld, and it does not reach the voice assistant;
  • it is not logged, at any log level, including DEBUG;
  • only a reply is kept, as an event on the consent record, with the words that were read as the reply.

When consent is given, the listening session is closed before capture starts, and transcription opens sessions of its own, so no audio heard while consent was awaited reaches the transcript.

What this means for processing. While consent is awaited the meeting audio is processed by the transcription engine, although nothing is kept apart from the reply events. With Amazon Transcribe, that audio is sent to the Amazon Transcribe streaming service in this deployment’s Region, as the meeting’s transcription would be, and it is billed and appears in the meeting’s cost estimate as Amazon Transcribe streaming. With the MicroVM engine it stays on the MicroVM, which runs, and is billed, from the time the VP is admitted rather than from when consent is given. Choose the mode with this in mind, and turn Read spoken replies off where no meeting audio may leave the Virtual Participant before consent.

Who is present

Section titled “Who is present”

The participant list the VP keeps on the record decides the each-participant rule, so it is never allowed to be shorter than the meeting:

  • Where the platform gives a list of names that accounts for everybody the attendee count shows (the Teams join through Azure Communication Services), the list is those names.
  • Where it gives only a count (the Chime, Zoom, Teams and Google Meet browser joins, and the Zoom Meeting SDK join), the list is the names the VP has seen since the count last went up, as the active speaker, as the speaker of a spoken reply, or as a chat sender, plus one Unidentified participant N for each seat no name accounts for. An unidentified participant can never consent, so the meeting is not granted until everybody present has spoken or written in the chat, and consented. When the count goes up, the names seen before stop counting, because one of them may have left in the meantime: everybody present has to be seen again (speaking is enough; their consent is already on the record). When it goes down while consent is still awaited, the same applies. While consent is granted it does not, because a departure cannot add anybody who has not consented.
  • Where it gives neither (the Webex browser join), the names seen can never be shown to be everybody, so one unidentified participant is always listed and the each-participant rule is never met, whether replies are typed or said. The detail line says so. Use the owner-attests rule on Webex.
  • Where the platform shows no chat sender (the Teams browser join), a consent typed in the chat cannot be attributed, but one said aloud (“LMA, I consent”) can, under the name the platform shows speaking. So the each-participant rule is met when every participant present has said their consent while the VP could see who was speaking, and the detail line adds that on this platform consent is given aloud. With Read spoken replies off it is never met there, and the detail line says to choose the owner-attests rule or turn the setting on.

The count is the one each platform handler already reads to leave a meeting it is alone in: whenever the count on the page changes on the Chime and Zoom browser joins (plus a check every 30 seconds on Zoom), every 3 seconds on the two SDK joins, every 20 seconds by default on the Teams browser join (the Virtual Participant section’s attendee poll interval), and every 20 seconds on the Google Meet join. One person leaving and another joining between two readings leaves the count unchanged, which the VP cannot see; the window is as long as the interval between readings.

The names are display names, as everywhere in the record. The VP’s own identities are never listed.

Evidence screenshots

Section titled “Evidence screenshots”

When the meeting’s status becomes granted (in notify, once the notice is recorded as posted), and on every objection, the VP takes a screenshot of the meeting page with the chat open, then, where the platform allows it, opens the participant list, takes a second one, and puts the chat back. The screenshots are taken from the meeting page itself, so nothing open in the live view covers them. Each is a JPEG stored in the recordings bucket under lma-consent-evidence/<meeting>/ and added to the record with addMeetingConsentEvidence, with a note of what it shows and when (“Meeting chat when Bo Chen objected”). For each chat screenshot the VP then asks for the vision-model check (verifyMeetingConsentEvidence) without waiting for it; a participant list on its own is not checked, because the check reads a chat for the notice and the replies. Neither the screenshots nor the check ever hold up the meeting: a failure is logged and the meeting carries on. A VP keeps at most 24 images per meeting, below the record’s 30, and takes no more after that. Before leaving after an objection it waits up to 30 seconds for that objection’s screenshots.

Not done yet

Section titled “Not done yet”
  • The notice is posted in the chat only, not spoken by the voice assistant, so a participant who cannot see the chat hears no request to reply.
  • Spoken replies have not been exercised in a live meeting on any join or with either engine yet. How reliably a short reply is heard, and how often it is attributed to the right speaker, depends on each platform’s speaker indicator and has not been measured.
  • Spoken replies are not read while the transcript is paused with LMA pause (nothing is sent to the engine then); replies in the chat still are.
  • The screen recording does not resume once capture has been withheld after it started (see Objections).

Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App

Section titled “Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App”

Stream Audio sends a meeting’s audio to the WebSocket transcriber, which creates the meeting. Neither the page nor the user’s browser can see the meeting’s participants or its chat, so the consent step is the LMA user’s own: they share the notice, and confirm that it was shared or, in opt-in, that everyone agreed. The transcriber records that confirmation on the meeting, as a backend role, and is the component that decides whether audio is transcribed and recorded. The Desktop Capture App uses the same protocol, described in the WebSocket streaming API, and the same rules; The Desktop Capture App below says where it differs.

The consent step

Section titled “The consent step”

When the page opens its connection to the transcriber it asks for the consent settings in force (a CONSENT_SETTINGS frame). With the mode Off, or a transcriber that does not answer, the page shows no step and streams as before. Otherwise a Meeting consent panel appears above the Start button with:

  • The notice for the mode in force (the consent request in opt-in), its placeholders filled with the meeting owner field and the meeting topic, and a Copy notice button for pasting it into the meeting’s chat.
  • The confirmation: in opt-in, Everyone in the meeting agreed to it being recorded and transcribed; in notify, I have told everyone in the meeting, with the notice above. With it, how: out loud, in the meeting chat, or in the invitation’s terms, and an optional note.
  • Capture evidence (optional): the browser asks which tab or window to share; one frame of it is kept as a JPEG and the capture stops at once. While streaming, the button takes a frame of the tab being streamed, with no prompt.
  • Participant objected, while streaming, with an optional name.

In opt-in, Start is refused until the confirmation is ticked and a method chosen. The page sends its consent step with every START, as mode Off when it has not read the settings yet (Start pressed straight after opening the page, or the settings unavailable), so the transcriber refuses an opt-in meeting the page did not ask about, rather than treating it as a client without a consent step; the page then reads the settings again and shows the step. In notify, streaming may start without them; the notice is then not recorded as posted, so the meeting’s status stays pending, which in notify still allows capture.

What the transcriber records, and when

Section titled “What the transcriber records, and when”

The confirmation travels in the consent field of the START frame. The meeting does not exist yet at that moment: the transcriber puts the meeting’s start on the Kinesis stream, and the call event processor creates its row a moment later. So the transcriber waits for the row, retrying for up to 30 seconds, reads the meeting’s owner, and then, if it is the signed-in user who streams, through the meeting consent API:

  1. starts the meeting’s consent in the deployment’s mode, naming the rule The meeting owner attests for opt-in, with the meeting owner field and the topic for the notice’s placeholders;
  2. records the notice as posted, if the user confirmed, with the channel the method implies (out loud: voice; chat: chat; invitation: invite) and the text as the page showed it;
  3. in opt-in, relays the attestation, which the record attributes to the meeting’s owner (the signed-in user who streams), with the method and the note, or a sentence saying how everyone agreed when there is no note;
  4. stores any evidence image under the meeting’s evidence folder, adds it to the record, and asks for it to be checked with the vision model (verifyMeetingConsentEvidence). The check is supporting evidence and does not change the status (see Checking evidence with a vision model); if the check fails, the image is kept unchecked. A meeting may send at most five images, each a JPEG or PNG of at most 3.75 MB; an image that is refused or could not be stored does not count towards the five.

After each step the transcriber tells the page what the record says, and the page shows it. The transcriber records the step after START rather than the page recording it itself, because the page cannot know when the meeting’s row exists and only a backend role may add evidence.

How capture is gated

Section titled “How capture is gated”
  • Notify: audio is transcribed and recorded from the start, as notify allows until somebody objects. If the consent record cannot be written, the meeting goes on and the page says so: CONSENT_NOT_RECORDED when the record could not be started, NOTICE_NOT_RECORDED when only the notice could not be recorded as posted.
  • Opt-in: nothing is transcribed or recorded until the record answers that the meeting may be captured, usually a second or two after Start. Audio sent before then is discarded, not buffered. If the record cannot be written — the meeting was not created in time, the API refused the attestation, or the transcriber has no API configured — the meeting is ended without anything having been captured, and the page says why.
  • An objection: Participant objected stops the page’s capture, and the transcriber stops transcribing and recording at once, before the objection is written, ends the meeting as if the page had sent END, and records the objection (under the name given, or A participant, with source voice). What was captured before the objection is kept: an objection stops capture, it does not delete what was captured while capture was allowed. A meeting whose record answers that it may not be captured is ended the same way.
  • A refused meeting (opt-in without the confirmation, or a deployment the page cannot satisfy) is not created at all: the transcriber answers with the reason and closes the connection.
  • Video: a desktop video stream for a meeting is refused while the meeting’s consent does not allow capture, and is finalized when an objection stops it.

An opt-in deployment whose rule is Each participant consents and that does not allow a different choice per meeting cannot be satisfied from Stream Audio or the Desktop Capture App, which have no participant list: the page or the app says so and Start is refused. Use a Virtual Participant for such meetings, or allow a per-meeting choice.

If the transcriber cannot read the consent settings when a meeting starts, it records the page’s step in the mode the page showed, and the meeting consent resolver, which reads the settings itself, decides: in either mode nothing is transcribed or recorded until the record answers that the meeting may be captured, and if the record refuses the step the meeting is ended. A client without a step is then captured, since nothing says that a step is needed.

The Desktop Capture App

Section titled “The Desktop Capture App”

The macOS and Windows apps show the same step in their panel (see Meeting consent in the Desktop Capture App), and the transcriber records and gates it exactly as for Stream Audio. The differences:

  • Reading the settings. The app connects to the transcriber only when a recording starts, so it asks for the settings on a short-lived connection of its own when the user signs in, when the panel opens, and at Start if it has no answer yet. A transcriber that does not answer within six seconds is treated as one that predates the consent frames: no step is shown. The app still sends its step with START, as mode Off, as Stream Audio does, so in an opt-in deployment the transcriber refuses the meeting and the app reads the settings again rather than being treated as a client without a consent step.
  • Evidence is one frame of the screen or window selected as the screen-video source in the app’s Settings, taken with the capture permission the app already holds (on macOS, ScreenCaptureKit, macOS 14 or later). It can be taken before Start, when it is sent once the meeting starts, or while recording, when it is sent at once.
  • Reconnects. The app sends a fresh START, with the same consent step, each time its connection drops and reconnects. The transcriber finds the meeting’s record already started and does not record the notice or the attestation a second time; in an opt-in meeting the first second or so of audio after each reconnect is discarded until the record answers again. A meeting somebody objected to is not resumed: the transcriber refuses it and the app stops.
  • Screen video of an opt-in meeting starts once the meeting’s consent allows capture, since the transcriber refuses a video stream before that.
  • The command line (--cli) prints the notice and takes the confirmation as --consent-method verbal|chat|invite_terms, with an optional --consent-note.

Clients without a consent step

Section titled “Clients without a consent step”

Clients that show no consent step — the browser extension, the Stream Audio embed (#/embed?component=stream-audio), Desktop Capture App builds from before the consent step, and scripts written against the streaming API — send START without a consent field. In Notify or Opt-in the transcriber treats such a meeting by the setting Streaming clients without a consent step:

  • Accept with a warning (the shipped default): the meeting is recorded and transcribed as before, with no consent record, and the transcriber logs a warning naming the meeting. This keeps existing desktop builds and integrations working while they are updated, but it means that in an opt-in deployment such a meeting is captured without anybody having confirmed consent. Its meeting has no consent record, and getMeetingConsent reports it as not allowed.
  • Refuse: the meeting is not created, recorded or transcribed; the client is sent a CONSENT_STATE frame with the code CONSENT_REQUIRED and the connection is closed. A client without the consent step does not read that frame either, so the browser extension and the Stream Audio embed show only that the connection closed, and each reconnect is refused again. Choose this only once every client in use shows the consent step, and not while the browser extension or the Stream Audio embed is in use.

What is stored

Section titled “What is stored”

The consent record is the Consent attribute of the meeting’s row in the event-sourcing table, so it is kept, shared and deleted with the meeting. It holds:

FieldMeaning
Mode, RuleThe meeting’s mode, and for opt-in its rule
Status, CaptureAllowedAs above, worked out on every write
NoticeTextThe notice or request for this meeting, placeholders filled
NoticePostedAt, NoticesWhen the notice was first posted; each posting, with its channel (chat, voice, banner or invite), text, time and who recorded it. The first posting and the latest 9 are kept
Participants, ParticipantsSeenWho is present now, as last listed, and everyone ever listed (the first 500)
EventsEvery reply and attestation in the order recorded: the participant’s display name, consent or objection, where it was read (chat, voice, ui for an entry a signed-in user made, or attestation), the words when sent, when it happened, when it was recorded and by whom
AttestationWho attested, how (verbal, chat or invite_terms), what they said, and when
EvidenceThe images kept: each one’s id, kind (screenshot or invitation), where it is stored, when it was taken, a note, the vision model’s check of it (Verification) when one ran, and when the image was deleted after its check (ImageDeletedAt) when screenshots are not kept
StatusHistoryEach change of status or of CaptureAllowed, with the time and reason; the latest 100 are kept
StartedAt, StartedBy, UpdatedAt, VersionWhen and by whom consent was started; when it last changed; a count that increases with every write

A participant list of more than 250 distinct names is refused, so a meeting larger than that cannot be granted under the Each participant consents rule; use The meeting owner attests for it. A meeting keeps at most 30 evidence images. Events are compacted towards 300: past that, older events are dropped, but never a participant’s latest reply or the latest attestation, so the status does not change by being compacted, and a meeting in which more than 300 names have replied keeps one event for each of them.

The record shares one database item, of at most 400 KB, with the rest of the meeting row, so it is also held to 150 KB as a whole. A write that would take it past that first sheds what the status does not depend on, in this order: replies that are no longer anybody’s latest, the words of replies, the older status history, the notices between the first and the latest, the names no longer present, what the evidence check read in each image, and finally each reply’s other details and each image’s note and check summary. A write that still would not fit is refused with a message saying so.

Evidence images are private objects in the recordings bucket, under lma-consent-evidence/<meeting>/, where <meeting> is the CallId with every character other than letters, digits, _ and . replaced by _ and any leading and trailing _ removed (the same rule as the key frames’ folder). Two CallIds that differ only in such characters, a b and a_b for example, share a folder, as they do for key frames. Only a backend role can add one, and only from that folder. getMeetingConsent signs an hour-long link to each image for whoever may read the meeting.

Deleting a meeting deletes its consent record, which is part of the meeting row, and every image in its evidence folder. A lifecycle rule on lma-consent-evidence/ also deletes the images after TranscriptionExpirationInDays, as for the key frames. The record itself is kept as long as the meeting record (MeetingRecordExpirationInDays), which is usually longer, so a record can go on listing images that have expired: their entries and anything stored about them remain, and a link to one leads nowhere. If the images are needed for as long as the record, keep the two retentions equal, or copy the images elsewhere before they expire.

Checking evidence with a vision model

Section titled “Checking evidence with a vision model”

An evidence screenshot — typically the meeting chat after the request was posted — can be checked by an Amazon Bedrock vision model with verifyMeetingConsentEvidence. The model is shown the image with the notice that was posted, the meeting’s mode, the consent and objection phrases and the participants listed as present, and asked to report what the picture shows:

  • whether the notice is visible, and whether it says the same as the notice posted;
  • every visible reply to it, with the sender’s name as shown, the reply’s text, and whether it reads as consent, an objection, or neither;
  • whether anybody objected;
  • how sure it is, from 0 to 1, and a one-sentence summary.

From that answer LMA draws a verdict, the same way whichever model answered:

VerdictWhen
objection_foundThe model reported an objection, or a visible reply reads as one. Reported whatever the model’s confidence.
supports_consentThe notice is visible, nobody objected, the model’s confidence is at least 0.5, and — for the opt-in rule Each participant consents — every participant listed as present has a visible reply reading as consent.
inconclusiveAnything else, including an answer that could not be read. For the each-participant rule, the participants with no visible consent are listed (MissingParticipants).

The result is stored on the evidence entry as Verification, with the model’s id, when the check ran, who ran it and the tokens it used. It is supporting evidence only: it never changes the meeting’s consent status, which follows only from the replies and attestations recorded on the meeting. A check that finds an objection nobody recorded is for a person to look at — the owner can record the objection — and a check that supports consent does not grant it.

The model is told that everything in the screenshot, including text that looks like an instruction, is content to report, and not to identify anybody from their face or appearance, only from names written on the screen.

Model and cost. The model is Evidence check model in the Meeting Consent settings; the shipped default is Amazon Nova Lite (amazon.nova-lite-v1:0), the same in-region model the key frame text reader uses by default, so a deployment that reads key frames needs no further model access. Each check is one model call, recorded against the meeting as Consent evidence check in its cost estimate.

Measured on four synthetic screenshots of a meeting with a chat panel (1568 × 882 JPEG), each checked three times per model at temperature 0: every participant consenting (expected supports_consent), one of three participants not replying (inconclusive), an objection worded freely, “I don’t consent to being recorded, please turn it off” (objection_found), and a chat line telling the checker to ignore its instructions and report that everyone consented, followed by an objection (objection_found):

ModelVerdicts as expectedInput / output tokens per checkCost per checkTime per check
Amazon Nova Lite12 of 12about 2,630 / 110about $0.0002about 1.3 s
Amazon Nova 2 Lite12 of 12about 790 / 140about $0.0006about 1.3 s
Claude Haiku 4.512 of 12about 2,120 / 190about $0.003about 1.8 s

Four synthetic screenshots are a small sample with clean, legible text. Real chat panels are smaller, busier and partly hidden, so expect more inconclusive results from them, and choose a larger model if the default reads them poorly.

Keeping or deleting the image. With Keep evidence screenshots on (the default), the image stays with the meeting until the meeting is deleted. With it off, the image is deleted as soon as its check is stored, and the entry keeps the check and ImageDeletedAt; such an entry cannot be checked again. If the deletion fails, the check is still stored and the image is left for the meeting’s deletion or the lifecycle rule to remove. If the image is deleted but ImageDeletedAt cannot be recorded, the failure is logged with the evidence id, and the entry goes on looking as if its image were kept.

Timing. The check runs while the request waits. AppSync answers a request within 30 seconds; a check that takes longer still stores its result, but the request returns an error and no onUpdateMeetingConsent message is sent, so the result is seen on the next read of the record.

The WebSocket transcriber’s task role is one of LMA’s backend roles. It is granted the six consent mutations it calls (startMeetingConsent, recordMeetingConsentNotice, recordMeetingConsentEvent, attestMeetingConsent, addMeetingConsentEvidence and verifyMeetingConsentEvidence), and getCall to read a meeting’s owner before writing anything, and nothing else on the API. It acts only for the meeting started on the connection a frame arrives on, and records a consent step only on a meeting owned by the signed-in user who started it, so a signed-in user can record consent, an objection or evidence only for their own meeting that they are streaming. A START with a consent step that names an existing meeting owned by somebody else is refused with MEETING_NOT_OWNED before anything is written for it, and the client is told to use another meeting name. If such a meeting is created while the step is being recorded, this connection’s capture of it is dropped as soon as its owner is read, with nothing written to its consent record and no end event or recording written for it.

These rules are applied by the meeting consent resolver on every request, as the meeting’s other edits are applied by theirs; a meeting the caller may not see is refused in the same way as one that does not exist.

API

Section titled “API”

All the operations are on the AppSync GraphQL API, with Cognito user pool or IAM authorization. Every mutation answers with a MeetingConsentUpdate (CallId, Owner, SharedWith, Consent), which is also what the onUpdateMeetingConsent(CallId) subscription carries. A subscriber receives the Consent fields the writer selected, so a writer should select the whole record; the fragment below does. A subscriber that shows the evidence images reads getMeetingConsent again on each message, because only that query signs their links.

Arguments that are checked:

  • Mode is off, notify or opt_in; Rule is each_participant or owner_attests, and is used only with opt_in. Omitted, each is the meeting’s own once its consent has started, and the deployment’s before that.
  • Starting consent again with the mode and rule already in force, or naming neither, returns the record as it is, without a write: a capture client that restarts keeps the meeting’s choice and its notice even if the deployment’s settings have changed since. NoticeText, LmaUser and MeetingName are read only when consent starts or its mode changes.
  • Starting it with a different mode or rule is refused once a notice, reply, attestation or evidence image has been recorded. Before that, the mode is changed and the change is added to the status history; the participants listed and when consent was first started are kept.
  • Every other mutation is refused until consent has been started.
  • A backend role’s reply names its Source, chat or voice. An attestation a backend role relays is recorded as the meeting owner’s; AttestedBy, when given, must name the owner.
  • Names are cut at 100 characters, reply and statement texts at 500, notices at 2,000, and an evidence key may be at most 256 characters.

Each write reads the meeting row and writes the record back on condition that nobody else wrote it in between, retrying a few times, so replies that arrive together are all kept. A write that keeps meeting another one is refused with a message to try again.

The rules that decide the status and the gate are in one Python module, lma_consent in the configuration layer (lma-ai-stack/source/lambda_layers/configuration_layer/lma_consent/), which the resolver uses and a Python capture client or script can import; it also holds the reply classifier described above.

Limitations

Section titled “Limitations”
  • Display names are not verified identities. Replies are matched to participants by the name the meeting platform shows. A participant can rename themselves, two people can show the same name, and a guest’s name is whatever they typed. A reply is recorded under the name its sender showed when it was sent, and two participants showing one name are counted as one.
  • Speech is read as words, and attributed by the speaker indicator. A spoken reply is only as reliable as its transcript; a reply in a language the transcription is not set up for may not be read at all. It is recorded under the name the platform showed speaking, which can be wrong when people speak over each other or the indicator lags, and is missing when nobody was shown speaking.
  • A model’s reading is supporting evidence. The evidence check reports what a vision model read in a screenshot. It can misread small or partly hidden text, and it never changes a meeting’s status.
  • What LMA records is evidence, not a determination. The record shows what was posted and what was replied, when; whether that amounts to consent where the meeting took place is for your organisation to decide.
  • A streamed meeting’s consent is the user’s word. Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App cannot see the participants or the meeting chat, so an opt-in meeting from either is granted on the LMA user’s attestation alone. An evidence screenshot is the only part of the record that shows what the participants saw or replied.
  • Not every capture method runs the flow yet. See Flows by capture method.
  • What the Virtual Participant can see differs by platform. Who is present and who sent a chat message or is speaking come from the meeting page, and not every platform shows all three; see What each platform supports.
  • The record is meeting data. It is kept and deleted with the meeting, and anyone the meeting is shared with can read it, including participants’ names and replies.
