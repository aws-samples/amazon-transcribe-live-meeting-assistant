## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Key frames of the shared screen.** A Virtual Participant recording meeting video now also keeps a key frame each time the shared screen changes to something new and holds still — each slide, document page or application view — stamped with the moment it first appeared on the transcript's clock and its position in the video recording. On Zoom and Teams only the time a screen share is on screen is sampled; on Webex and Amazon Chime, which show no share signal, every second is judged against stricter thresholds. The Desktop Capture App's screen video gets key frames too, judged by the WebSocket transcriber as the video arrives. Moving content such as camera tiles is left out of the comparison, so a camera gallery produces no frames. Sampling costs well under 1% of a vCPU on the Virtual Participant and about 1.4% per desktop video stream on the transcriber, and each key frame about 76 KB of storage. Key frames are stored on the meeting (a new `ADD_VIDEO_KEYFRAME` call data stream event, `getVideoKeyframes` query and `onAddVideoKeyframe` subscription), are listed to the same people as the transcript, are shared and deleted with the meeting, and are kept as long as the transcript (`TranscriptionExpirationInDays`). They follow the **Record meeting video** toggle and, for the desktop app, its **Also record screen video** setting. The meeting page shows them as a **Shared screen** strip of thumbnails that grows during the meeting: selecting one moves the transcript to that time and, after the meeting, plays the video recording from there. The images are shown through hour-long links that `getVideoKeyframes` signs after checking the caller's access to the meeting. The meeting assistant has a new `get_meeting_frames` tool that reads the frames a question is about with what was said while each was on screen, and passes the pictures to the model (every offered model except Amazon Nova Micro), so it can answer "summarize that slide", "translate the last slide into French" or "show me the last two slides" — with the frames shown in the meeting page's chat, and described in words in the meeting chat and by voice. The pictures are billed as input tokens in the Meeting Assistant cost line, with their number listed. See [Meeting Video and Key Frames](docs/meeting-video.md). (#887, #889, #890)
- **Meetings can carry an agenda.** Each meeting now has an Agenda panel on its page that shows the agenda as a numbered list, with where it came from and who last changed it, and lets the owner, anyone the meeting is shared with, or an Admin add or edit it while the meeting runs and after it ends. The agenda is stored on the meeting record (`Agenda`, `AgendaSource`, `AgendaUpdatedBy`, `AgendaUpdatedAt`) and written through a new `updateCallAgenda` GraphQL mutation that applies the same access rule as the summary and speaker-name edits; changes reach open meeting pages through the existing `onUpdateCall` subscription. An agenda can also be given when a Virtual Participant is started or scheduled — in the new **Agenda** field of the Virtual Participant form, which **Paste Meeting Invitation** fills from the invitation, or through the new optional `agenda` argument of the MCP `start_meeting_now` and `schedule_meeting` tools — and the meeting then has it from the moment it is created. The Strands meeting assistant has a new `get_meeting_agenda` tool and guidance for progress checks — each agenda item reported as covered, in progress or remaining, with the transcript times where it was discussed — reached from a new **AGENDA CHECK** Meeting Assist chat button (added to the shipped defaults, so deployments without a custom button list get it), from the voice assistant ("Hey Alex, what's left on the agenda?") and from `LMA ask` in the meeting chat, where the agenda is available to everyone in the meeting, guests included. An agenda somebody states in the meeting is also read from the transcript — automatically, once, about five minutes into a meeting with no agenda, and on demand with a **Derive agenda** button — and stored only when it was stated: the model must quote the words it was stated in, the quote must be in the transcript, and each item must be drawn from it; each pass is metered as **Agenda derivation** in the meeting's cost estimate. When a meeting has an agenda, its summary gains an **AGENDA COVERAGE** section marking each item covered, partly covered or not covered; the section is a new shipped summary prompt using a new `{agenda}` placeholder, and is left out for meetings without an agenda. See [Meeting Agenda](docs/meeting-agenda.md). (#880, #884, #885, #886)

- **Questions typed in the meeting chat are answered there by the meeting assistant.** `LMA ask <question>` (or `@LMA <question>`) in the meeting chat sends the question to the Strands meeting assistant with the meeting's transcript as context, and the Virtual Participant posts the answer in the chat as plain single-line text, with link addresses kept in full, split into at most three messages within each platform's length. It works on Zoom, Teams, Webex and Chime, with or without a voice assistant, whenever **MeetingAssistService** is a Strands option, and `LMA help` lists it then. Because the whole meeting reads the answer, chat questions are answered from the current meeting's transcript and web search only, without the document knowledge base, meeting history, MCP servers or browser controls; any participant may ask, and there is no separate switch to turn this off; each participant may ask once every 20 seconds, and questions are not answered while the transcript is paused. See [Asking the Meeting Assistant from Chat](docs/virtual-participant.md#asking-the-meeting-assistant-from-chat). (#871, #883)
- **An estimated cost for every meeting, itemised by what it was spent on.** LMA now records usage where it happens — Amazon Transcribe seconds (or ASR MicroVM time), the tokens of every Bedrock call for summaries, speaker naming, the meeting assistant, the Nova Sonic voice assistant and page automation, the Virtual Participant's Fargate or MicroVM compute, and Comprehend sentiment — as metering records against the meeting, and prices them with a table of public list prices dated 2026-10-28 for us-east-1 and us-west-2. A new **Estimated cost** section on the meeting page, and `Query.getCallCost`, show the estimate line by line, with the price date and source, the lines it could not price, and what it leaves out (always-on infrastructure, Knowledge Base queries, third-party services). Admins can change any price through `getCallPricing` / `updateCallPricing`; the table is the `call_pricing` section of the configuration store. Meetings held before the upgrade have no records and read as not metered. See [Meeting Cost Estimate](docs/meeting-cost-estimate.md). (#849, #854)

- **An AI troubleshooting agent that reads your deployment's own logs to explain a failure.** Diagnosing LMA means reading several CloudWatch log groups in the right order: a meeting that produced no transcript looks identical whether the WebSocket upgrade was refused, the speech engine declined the stream, the Kinesis consumer threw, or the Virtual Participant never got into the meeting — and the evidence that distinguishes those sits in four different log groups whose names cannot be worked out from the stack name you typed. A new read-only agent, built on the Strands Agents SDK and Amazon Bedrock like the Meeting Assistant, does that reading and answers with a root cause, at most three recommendations, and the log groups, streams, lines and records the cause rests on. Every log line a search returns names the log group it came from, and the Evidence section quotes that name rather than one the model recalls; anything the model writes before the answer's first heading is removed. It is told to answer "Not determined" and name the evidence it was missing rather than offer a likely cause, because a diagnosis you cannot check is worse than none. Four tools: read the stack's CloudFormation parameters, discover the log groups, query them with CloudWatch Logs Insights (so it can count and correlate, not just grep), and read one Virtual Participant record. On a stack upgraded from an earlier release it also finds the MicroVM log groups that release wrote to, `/aws/lambda-microvms/<stack>-vp` and `-asr`, which the upgrade leaves in place and which hold the output of any Virtual Participant that ran on the pre-upgrade image, and it searches them as well as the current `-vp-logs` and `-asr-logs` groups. For a Virtual Participant that was ended from outside — through the end operation from the UI, SDK or CLI, or by the task reaper after its ECS task stopped — it reports the reason recorded on the row (`endReason`, or the reaper's `errorMessage`) and `endedAt` as the reason it ended, corroborated by the participant's log (such a participant writes no `VP exit:` line), and says so when the row's status is stale because a progress update landed after the end. Its tool results steer it to search an id and every marker it is looking for in one call, over a window reaching back to when the participant was created, rather than spending its tool-call limit on one search per term. **⚠️ There is no entry point in the web UI yet** — invoke the `STRANDS-<stack>-Troubleshooting` function with the AWS CLI or SDK, using the new `TroubleshootingAgentFunctionArn` stack output; the stack grants `lambda:InvokeFunction` on it to no principal, so unless you hold it through a broad account-level policy, access has to be granted deliberately. The agent has its own IAM role, separate from the Meeting Assistant's, scoped to this deployment's log groups, its two tables and its own stack tree; it reads and cannot change, restart or redeploy anything, the only non-read grants being the one GraphQL mutation it narrates progress through, cancelling a log query it started, and writing its own logs and traces. Its system prompt lives in DynamoDB rather than in code: set a `systemPrompt` attribute on the `CustomTroubleshootingAgentPrompt` item (the new `TroubleshootingAgentCustomPrompt` output links straight to it) and the change applies to the next diagnosis with no redeployment. Every run is bounded by an explicit tool-call limit, token budget, wall-clock budget and per-tool result caps, each adjustable as an environment variable on the function for a temporary change — they are not stack parameters, so a stack update restores the defaults — and a failed run is reported rather than retried. A run that reaches one of those limits still ends with the three-section answer — the model's own, or one saying nothing was concluded and naming the limit. No new stack parameters; it uses the model named by `MeetingAssistServiceBedrockModelID` and applies `BedrockGuardrailId` when one is configured. One deployment-visible side effect: the agent shares the Strands Agents SDK Lambda layer with the meeting assistant, so that layer is now published on every deployment rather than only on ones whose `MeetingAssistService` is Strands-backed — expect a new `<stack>-strands-layer` version to appear even if you have not selected a Strands option. See [AI Troubleshooting Agent](docs/troubleshooting-agent.md). (#810, #817, #819, #844)

- **LMA now publishes its own metrics for the meeting lifecycle, and alarms on two of them.** Seven CloudWatch metrics answer one question end to end — a meeting was asked for, did a transcript come out of it, how long did that take, and was anything dropped on the way: `MeetingsStarted`, `MeetingsEnded`, `TranscriptSegmentsWritten` and `TranscriptSegmentWriteFailed` from the Call Event Processor, and `StreamingSessionsStarted`, `TimeToFirstTranscriptSegmentMs` and `StreamingSessionsWithoutTranscript` from the WebSocket transcriber. They are published in the CloudWatch namespace `<stack-name>`, so two deployments in one account stay separate, and the operational dashboard gains a band that graphs them. Two of the deployment's alarms read them, taking it to 14 in all (one fewer where the stale-meeting reaper is switched off): **`transcript-segment-write-failures`**, for a transcript segment that did not reach the meeting page, and **`meetings-without-transcript`**, for audio sessions that streamed for at least thirty seconds and produced nothing. Both watch conditions that nothing else reports — the audio session closes normally and the meeting page shows a gap, or an empty transcript, with no error on it. There are no new stack parameters, and `EnableOperationalAlarms` switches these alarms off with the rest. The metrics are written as embedded-metric-format log records rather than through `PutMetricData`, so no IAM role gained a metric-writing permission and every datapoint is traceable to the log event that produced it; the cost is about $2.10 per stack per month in us-east-1 for seven custom metrics, or less if the account has not used its ten free custom metrics elsewhere. The two alarms are included in the alarm figure given in the entry below. The Call Event Processor's `POWERTOOLS_METRICS_NAMESPACE` is also set to the stack name now, so the two names for one namespace cannot disagree; nothing in the shipped function publishes through AWS Lambda Powertools, so only code added to that function itself is affected. The MicroVM ASR and Meeting Assistant components still publish nothing, and the Virtual Participant publishes only the Nova Sonic quota metric described in the voice-assistant entry below; all three remain diagnosable from their log groups. See [Monitoring & Alerting](docs/monitoring.md#metrics-lma-publishes). (#812)

- **Voice assistants keep working when the account runs out of Nova Sonic streams, and the dashboard shows when that happens.** Amazon Bedrock allows an account 20 concurrent Nova Sonic streams per region, a limit that cannot be raised and that every Virtual Participant in the account shares: a wake-phrase assistant holds one while it is activated, an always-listening one holds one for the whole meeting, and a translator holds two or three. Until now a wake-phrase assistant whose stream was refused was activated anyway with a session that could neither hear nor answer, and ignored the next wake phrase until its activation window ran out; an always-listening one retried every second. Now a refused wake phrase is retried three more times with a randomised backoff, about eight seconds at most, and if the stream still cannot be opened the assistant says nothing and the next wake phrase tries again; an always-listening assistant retries with a backoff that grows to 30 seconds. Each refusal writes a `[nova-throttle]` line to the Virtual Participant log and is counted in a new `NovaSonicStreamsThrottled` metric, graphed on the operational dashboard; Bedrock's own `InvocationThrottles`, which the `bedrock-throttling` alarm already reads, counts the same refusals account-wide. The metric adds one custom metric to the stack's bill, about $0.30 a month in us-east-1. See [Nova Sonic Concurrency Quota](docs/voice-assistant.md#nova-sonic-concurrency-quota) for how many meetings the quota allows. (#878)

- **A unified configuration store, with the transcript summary prompts, the Nova Sonic voice assistant settings, the Meeting Assist chat buttons and the Transcription Engine switches served from it.** LMA's runtime configuration has grown up one store at a time: six separate DynamoDB tables and SSM parameters, each with its own seeding custom resource, its own resolver and its own idea of how long a value may be cached. This release adds the single store five of them move into (the sixth, the MCP server list, stays a table of its own) — one `ConfigurationTable` holding a deployment baseline, an administrator's profile as a delta over it, and a pointer naming the profile in force — plus one shared accessor: a Python one, used by the Lambda functions, and a TypeScript one, used by the Virtual Participant. How long a value may be reused is declared by the section it belongs to rather than chosen where it is read, in three tiers: no cache with a consistent read for anything promised to apply at once or bearing consent, 30 seconds for per-meeting switches, and 300 seconds with a change fingerprint for prompts and model ids. The **transcript summary prompts** are the first store migrated, and saving them from the Transcript Summary page is now conditional on the version the page loaded: when two administrators have it open at once, the second save is refused with a message to reload rather than replacing the first. **No action is required on upgrade if you edit the summary prompts from the Transcript Summary page.** ⚠️ **If you edit them in the DynamoDB console instead, that route stops working:** the summariser no longer reads `CustomSummaryPromptTemplates` in the old table, and in the new `ConfigurationTable` the prompts are stored compressed, so an attribute added there by hand has no effect. Make prompt edits on the Transcript Summary page from now on. Otherwise, the first deployment that includes this change carries any customised prompts across from the previous table, realigned onto this release's section numbering. The one case to check afterwards is the new **`ConfigurationProfileOrigin`** stack output: if it reads `created-previous-table-unreadable`, the prompts you had customised were not carried across and have to be applied again from the Transcript Summary page. The value is stamped when the profile is created and is unchanged by later deployments or saves, so the answer is still there however long after the upgrade you look. The Transcript Summary page now stores only the sections you change, so a later release that improves a prompt you never edited still reaches you. Deleting a shipped section on the page now turns it off, by storing it as `NONE`, which is what setting its prompt to `NONE` has always done; **Reset to Defaults** brings every shipped section back, and **Cancel** now discards the edits on the page instead of saving them. ⚠️ **Action required only if you read the summary prompt templates through the GraphQL API:** `Query.getLLMPromptTemplate` is now served on Cognito user-pool authentication only, to members of the **Admin** group, matching the mutation that saves them, so a client reading it with SigV4 must switch to a user-pool token; and its JSON payload now holds the prompts plus the record's version and who last saved it, rather than the whole DynamoDB item. Nothing in LMA itself reads it that way, and the web UI is unaffected. ⚠️ **Action required only if you use the LAMBDA summary option with a function that validates its input:** **Regenerate** now invokes that function with `"RefreshConfiguration": true` alongside the `CallId`, so a schema that rejects unknown fields must allow it; a function that ignores fields it does not recognise needs no change. The **Nova Sonic voice assistant settings** are the second store migrated: the Nova Sonic Configuration page saves them into the same profile, and the Virtual Participant reads them from it when its voice assistant starts, with no cache, so a saved change still applies to the next meeting. **No action is required on upgrade if you edit them from the Nova Sonic Configuration page**: the first deployment that includes this change carries the settings you saved there across, and the new **`ConfigurationSectionAdoption`** stack output says so with `nova_sonic_config=adopted-from-previous-table` (or `nothing-to-adopt`, or, on later deployments, `already-present`). If it reads `nova_sonic_config=previous-table-unreadable`, your settings were not carried across and the shipped ones apply; the next deployment that runs the configuration seeder tries again, or save them from the page. ⚠️ **If you edit the `CustomNovaSonicConfig` item in the DynamoDB console instead, that route stops working**, as for the summary prompts: the `DefaultNovaSonicConfig` and `CustomNovaSonicConfig` stack outputs now link to the configuration table's records, where the settings are stored compressed. The page's save is conditional on the version it loaded in the same way as the summary prompts' — and because every configuration page saves into one profile, a save is also refused, with the same message to reload, when another administrator saved a different configuration page in between. ⚠️ **Action required only if you read the Nova Sonic settings through the GraphQL API:** `Query.getNovaSonicConfig` keeps both of its authorization modes, but its JSON payload now holds the settings plus the record's version and who last saved it, rather than the whole DynamoDB item with its `NovaSonicConfigId`, `*Information*` and `description` attributes. The **Meeting Assist chat buttons** are the third: the Edit Chat Buttons dialog saves them into the same profile, conditional on the version it loaded, and every signed-in user's chat panel still reads them as before; on upgrade they are carried across the same way and reported as `chat_buttons=...` in `ConfigurationSectionAdoption`, and editing `CustomChatButtonConfig` in the DynamoDB console likewise stops having any effect. `Query.getChatButtonConfig` still answers with one record's `N#Label` buttons, plus a `Version` key a client that keeps only `N#Label` keys already ignores. The **Transcription Engine switches** (the on-demand ASR engine's three runtime settings) are the fourth: saved from the page into the same profile, conditional on the version it loaded, carried across on upgrade as `asr_engine=...`, and read by the WebSocket transcriber at meeting start with the same 30-second reuse as before and by the Virtual Participant through `Query.getAsrConfig`, which now also returns a `Version` field. **Every save into the store records a revision**: who saved it, taken from the verified token; when; which sections it changed; and an optional `Comment`, which every configuration save mutation now accepts. Bodies are kept in a new versioned `ConfigurationBucket`, encrypted with the stack's key, and indexed in the configuration table. Each profile keeps its 20 newest revisions, plus its current one and any labelled one, and a save that changes nothing records nothing. Admins can list, read and compare revisions (`listConfigurationRevisions`, `getConfigurationRevision`, `diffConfigurationRevisions`), label them, and restore one with `restoreConfigurationRevision`. A restore only moves forward: it saves the old body as a new revision, so the state it replaced can itself be restored. It puts back the whole profile, every configuration page's settings, and is conditional on the version read. A save is refused rather than made if the state it would replace cannot be recorded. The new **Configuration ▸ Configuration History** page lists the revisions, shows any one of them, compares two setting by setting, and restores one with an optional comment. **No action is required on upgrade**: the profile the upgrade creates, holding everything carried across from the previous tables, is revision 1, and the new **`ConfigurationRevisionHistory`** stack output reads `custom=present`. If it reads `custom=not-started`, the history could not be written at deploy time. The deployment still succeeds, the first save from a configuration page records the profile instead, and the configuration seeder's log says why. A rollback removes the bucket together with the configuration table, under the same `EnableDataRetentionOnDelete` rule. See the [Unified Configuration Store](docs/configuration-store.md) guide. (#813, #848, #850, #851, #879, #882)

- **Operational alerts, alarms and a dashboard, deployed with the stack.** Every deployment now creates an SNS topic for operational alerts, 14 CloudWatch alarms that notify it, and a CloudWatch dashboard named `<stack-name>-<region>`, linked from the new `OperationalDashboardConsoleUrl` output. The alarms watch the pipeline end to end — Kinesis writes and consumer lag, the Call Event Processor, transcriber and Virtual Participant health, AppSync, Amazon Bedrock throttling, Amazon Transcribe quota headroom and the stale-meeting reaper, plus the two reading the application metrics described above — and each carries a description written as the first few steps of a runbook, so the alert email is usually enough to know what to check. **⚠️ Action required:** the email subscription created for your `AdminEmail` address starts in `PendingConfirmation` and delivers nothing until somebody clicks the link SNS sends to that address, so a stack deployed from a pipeline begins with no working subscriber. Two new parameters: **`EnableOperationalAlarms`** (default `true`) and **`TranscribeConcurrentStreamAlarmThreshold`** (default `20`, which is 80% of the default account quota of 25 — raise it whenever you raise the quota). Expect about $0.60 per stack per month in us-east-1 for the alarms, and one ALARM→OK pair from the stale-meeting-reaper alarm on a freshly created stack, which is how an alarm that watches for an absence of invocations behaves before the first run lands. See [Monitoring & Alerting](docs/monitoring.md). (#796)

- **A meeting summary can now be regenerated on demand, or written by hand.** A summary was generated once, when the meeting ended, and then went stale — somebody named the separated speaker voices so the transcript reads "Sarah Lee" where it read `spk_0`, or an admin reworded the summary prompt templates, and the stored summary still reflected neither. **Regenerate** on the Meeting Summary panel re-runs summarisation against the transcript and the prompt templates as they are now, through the same summariser the end-of-meeting summary uses (the Bedrock one, or your own function under the LAMBDA option). Under the BEDROCK option it also rewrites the copy of the transcript and summary held in S3 for the Bedrock Knowledge Base, which is a snapshot taken when the summary runs and which the Meeting Assistant's meeting-history search reads — so regenerating is now the way to get renamed speakers into that copy; it becomes searchable at the next scheduled Knowledge Base sync, which runs every 15 minutes. Under the LAMBDA option that copy is refreshed only if your own function writes it. Summarising calls the model once per prompt template and takes longer than a web request allows, so the run happens in the background: the panel shows **Regenerating** and the new summary arrives by itself, in every open page. A second run cannot start while one is going, in another tab or for another user, and **a run that fails leaves the previous summary exactly as it was** rather than replacing it with an error. Separately, **Edit** opens the summary as Markdown and **Save summary** stores it, which replaces it everywhere the stored summary is read — the page, the download, the Word and email exports and the embeddable panel. It does not reach the Knowledge Base copy described above, since only a summary run writes that, so the Meeting Assistant's meeting-history search keeps returning the generated text. **Edit** becomes available once a meeting has ended, because the end-of-meeting summary would otherwise replace what you wrote -- and that summary lands a few minutes after the meeting is marked as ended, so wait for it to appear before editing; an edit saved before it arrives is replaced by it. A hand-written summary is marked **Edited**, and regenerating one asks first, because the edited text cannot be recovered. Both controls are for the meeting's owner, a user it is shared with, or an admin — the same rule that governs who may name its speakers. No new stack parameters. See [Regenerating and Editing a Summary](docs/transcript-summarization.md#regenerating-and-editing-a-summary). (#779)

- **Speaker names are now found automatically, from what people say.** Most meetings open with people introducing themselves to each other, so the Speakers panel's new **Identify speakers** button reads the transcript and names the separated voices from that — and it runs by itself every 45 seconds while a meeting is live with voices still unnamed, and once when the meeting ends, before the summary, so the summary reads names rather than voice ids. Two kinds of evidence are used and deliberately not treated alike: someone saying who they are ("Hi everyone, I'm Sarah Lee") is applied as a name, while someone being addressed by name ("That's a good point, Sarah") is only ever offered as a **suggestion** with **Confirm** and **Not \<name\>** beside it, because that inference depends on the turn boundaries being right and speaker separation does sometimes put the start of one turn on the previous voice. A suggested name is shown with a dotted underline in the transcript so it never reads as settled; rejecting one also stops it being suggested again. Names you typed or locked are never changed by an automatic pass. The model is asked only for a name, the kind of evidence, and the numbered turn holding it — the attribution is then recomputed from the transcript, so a name attached to the wrong voice is discarded rather than applied. Voices are named under the default channel labels and also under a label you typed yourself (the Stream Audio participant name, or your address on a shared microphone) once speaker separation has split it into two or more voices; a Virtual Participant channel carrying several attendees' roster names is left as it is. A default label (`Other Participant`, the desktop app's `Other participants`, or `Me`) counts as unnamed even on an unseparated channel, so the automatic passes keep running until it is named. Requires Amazon Bedrock access to the model named by `BedrockModelId`; no new stack parameters. See [Finding the names automatically](docs/transcription-and-translation.md#finding-the-names-automatically).

- **Speaker names for separated voices, on a new Speakers panel on the meeting page.** Speaker separation can tell voices apart but cannot know who they are, so a conference-room meeting — or any meeting joined without a Virtual Participant — has until now read `Other Participant (spk_0)`. The panel lists one row per voice with its turn count and talk time; typing a name relabels **every segment that voice produced**, the ones already on screen included, and the name reaches the meeting summary, the Meeting Assistant and the transcript download as well as the transcript itself. (The copy of the transcript written to S3 for the Bedrock Knowledge Base is a snapshot taken when the summary runs, so naming speakers after that point needs the summary regenerating to refresh it.) Clicking a speaker name in the transcript opens the same rename inline. It works with either transcription engine, and on a meeting with no separation at all, where it names the channel instead. Also available: merging two voice ids onto one person (for when the engine split someone in two), locking a name, an "only this segment" override for a turn attributed to the wrong voice, and an optional paste-in of a meeting invite's attendee list to pick names from — optional because there is no automatic attendee list for a browser or desktop-app meeting. Names are stored on the meeting and applied when the transcript is read, so a rename is immediate and reversible and the engine's own voice id is never overwritten. Anyone who can view a meeting sees the names; its owner, a user it is shared with, or an admin can change them. See [Naming the Voices](docs/transcription-and-translation.md#naming-the-voices).

- **Voice enrollment: people can enrol their own voice and be labelled by name from their first word.** Off by default, and turning it on means storing biometric data about people — read [Voice Enrollment](docs/voice-enrollment.md) before you do. Set `EnableVoiceEnrollment=true` and an **Enrol My Voice** page appears for every user: they read the consent terms in full, tick a box and press a button, and only then record about 20–30 seconds of a fixed script. The recordings are used to compute a voiceprint and are then discarded — never written to S3 or a table — and from then on meetings label their speech with their name instead of `spk_0`. Consent is first-party and nobody can give it for anybody else: an admin can revoke on somebody's behalf, and there is no action anywhere that enrols a person. Withdrawal is immediate, needs nobody's approval, and deletes the voiceprint; names already written into past transcripts stay as ordinary editable text. Every consent, re-consent, withdrawal and expiry is appended to a separate log that the application has no permission to rewrite, and which holds no biometric data, so a withdrawal can destroy the voiceprint while preserving the record that consent was given and withdrawn. Consent lasts `VoiceEnrollmentRetentionDays` (default 365) and then lapses, deleting the voiceprint; `VoiceEnrollmentTermsVersion` asks everybody to agree again, and so does editing the terms text — including by changing the retention period, which the terms state. Both enrolling and matching need `EnableMicrovmAsr=true`, because that engine computes the speaker embeddings and Amazon Transcribe exposes none; with enrolment on and that engine off, the portal says the engine is missing. **Two limits worth knowing before you enable it:** only people who already have an account in this deployment can enrol, so an external participant or a contractor in a meeting room is not covered; and a name that came from a voiceprint is not yet distinguishable in the UI from one somebody typed, so correcting a wrong match is an ordinary rename on the Speakers panel. While `EnableVoiceEnrollment` is `false` the tables, the portal and the matching step do not exist in the deployment at all.

- **A Virtual Participant can tell apart several people behind one attendee, on either transcription engine.** A VP names speakers from the meeting roster, which is exact for an ordinary remote attendee and useless for a conference room: everybody sharing one speakerphone shares one roster entry, so a whole room arrived as a single speaker. **Virtual Participant voice separation** on **Configuration ▸ Transcription Engine** now labels the distinct voices behind one attendee as `Conference Room (spk_0)`, `Conference Room (spk_1)` and so on — the roster name for who the platform reports, the voice id for which voice within it — and those can then be named on the Speakers panel. The name always comes from the meeting platform; on Amazon Transcribe a voice id is shown only with the attendee it has coincided with for the most speech so far, and with nobody else at the same time. If that majority moves to another attendee during the meeting, rows already written keep the suffix they were given. (The other two naming routes do not apply to a VP: naming from what people say only considers placeholder speaker names, and voiceprints are seeded only into streaming sessions.) The switch previously had no effect unless the deployment had also moved Virtual Participants onto the on-demand engine, because only that path read it; on Amazon Transcribe the per-item speaker labels were requested and discarded. Still off by default, since the roster name is the better label for a normal attendee. Measured on four voices mixed onto one channel, the on-demand engine separated them better than Amazon Transcribe (3 labels at 79% time-weighted purity against 2 at 57%), so prefer the on-demand engine for conference rooms — on Amazon Transcribe each label straddled more than one person, and naming such a row attaches a real name to a large share of somebody else's words. Labels also arrive only when a segment finalises, and Amazon Transcribe caps a result at about thirty seconds, so the live transcript can show one roster name and no voice ids for that long before the row splits; and separation is least reliable for people sitting away from a shared microphone. See [Several people behind one attendee](docs/virtual-participant.md#several-people-behind-one-attendee) and [How Speaker Names Are Assigned](docs/virtual-participant.md#how-speaker-names-are-assigned).

- **Advisory AI pull request review, for contributors.** Every non-draft pull request against `develop` can now get an automated review posted as a comment: a new GitHub Actions workflow runs Claude Code over the diff using this repository's own review criteria (`.claude/skills/pr-review.md`, the same file the interactive `review <url>` workflow uses), with a GitLab counterpart for the internal mirror. It is **advisory** — it approves nothing and gates nothing; `code_checks` and `srt_security_review` remain the only gates, and the job is kept out of every required-check set. Three controls bound what it costs: a review is cancelled when a newer push supersedes it, a given head commit is reviewed at most once, and drafts are skipped. Because this repository is public and the comment is published on it, a security finding is written as the file, the line and the control that is needed; that contract is the control, and the harness additionally scans the composed comment as a backstop and withholds the whole review rather than publish something that does not match. It is **inert until a maintainer runs two setup commands** (deploy an IAM role, set one repository variable); with neither run, the job reports a skip and exits. Nothing about a deployment changes. See [Advisory AI pull request review](scripts/pr_review/README.md).

- **Why a Virtual Participant's meeting ended is now recorded in a form you can query.** Each VP record gains an `endTrigger` field naming which branch the VP exited through, from a fixed set — `pre-join:not-admitted`, `acs-not-admitted`, `attendees-left`, `meetingTimeout`, `error:renderer-crashed` and so on — written alongside the prose already shown in the UI. Until now that detail existed only in the container's log, so answering "why did joins fail today" meant reading logs for every meeting; it is now a query over the Virtual Participant table. Most values are fixed strings; four forms are a fixed prefix plus a detail the platform supplied, and whatever follows the first colon is stored as a lower-case hyphenated slug so those group by prefix rather than landing in groups of one. `endReason` — which `endVirtualParticipant` has always written — is now readable through the API too. See [The status detail line](docs/virtual-participant.md#the-status-detail-line).

- **The voice assistant can now be told to stop talking.** Saying "Hey Alex, stop talking" stops it — no closing summary, no offer of further help. **The assistant going quiet is the signal, not a spoken acknowledgement:** it often answers "Okay" first and LMA gives the model room to do so — including when the model says the word after asking to be muted, in which case its reply is played if it starts within 1.5 seconds of that request, for at most 1.5 seconds of audio, so a longer continuation of the interrupted answer is still cut off — but that word is best-effort and is absent whenever the model does not ask to mute itself promptly, because LMA then silences the assistant itself. Silence is the part that is guaranteed. It works in the default `normal` meeting mode as well as in `group`, and under either activation mode, where before it depended on the assistant happening to be in `group` mode on an `always_active` deployment. Three mechanisms act together so a stop does not rest on any one of them: Nova Sonic is given a `mute` tool with a system prompt that treats a request to stop as a request for silence rather than as the preface to a new request, and is the only one of the three that can speak the acknowledgement; LMA also watches completed transcript segments for a stop request addressed to the assistant, which is what makes silence certain, and which briefly holds its own mute so the tool call has room to arrive first; and speaking over the assistant still cuts its current utterance short. Each request writes a few `[stop-request]` lines to the Virtual Participant's CloudWatch log stream naming the route and the timings, so a missing acknowledgement can be explained rather than guessed at; an acknowledgement spoken after the model's request to be muted is recorded as `action=ack-started` followed by `action=ack-played` or `action=ack-capped`. Transcript matching is deliberately strict — the utterance has to name the assistant by wake phrase and carry no other request — so "Hey Alex, can you stop the recording and summarize the last hour?" is answered rather than obeyed. Being asked to stop silences the assistant until it is addressed again, not for the rest of the meeting: the next wake phrase brings it back. Group mode keeps its own muted-until-addressed behaviour, except that a wake phrase which is itself a request to stop no longer unmutes it. Translator mode is unchanged and is still paused and resumed only by its configured trigger phrases. On **ElevenLabs** the transcript path and barge-in apply but the tool and prompt do not, because that agent's persona lives in the ElevenLabs dashboard rather than in LMA — add the one-word instruction to the agent's own system prompt there if you want the same brevity. No new stack parameters. See [Asking the Assistant to Stop Talking](docs/voice-assistant.md#asking-the-assistant-to-stop-talking).

- **The voice assistant can now be switched between assistant and translator mode from the meeting chat, without restarting the meeting.** The meeting mode came from the Nova Sonic Configuration page and the activation mode from the `VoiceAssistantActivationMode` stack parameter, both read once when the Virtual Participant started — so a meeting that turned out to need an interpreter could not have one. Typing `LMA translate` in the meeting chat now switches the assistant into translator mode for the rest of that meeting, and `LMA translate French` (or `español`, `Deutsch`: any of the seven languages Nova 2 Sonic speaks) also sets the pair to the meeting's transcription language and the one named, for that meeting only; the configured default is not changed. Naming two languages (`LMA translate English to French`) is answered with a reminder to name one, and a configured pair is compared without regard to case. `LMA assist` switches back, restoring the activation mode that translator or listen mode replaced, so a wake-phrase meeting returns to the wake phrase unless someone typed `LMA always` in between (a `LMA wake` declined while translating is applied then). `LMA listen` puts the assistant into the configuration's group mode, quiet until addressed (`LMA group` is accepted too), and `LMA always` / `LMA wake` change when it is listening. Anyone in the meeting may issue these, like the existing `LMA leave`, and each is answered in chat; a command has to be the whole message, so ordinary chat that mentions LMA is left alone and the existing `LMA leave` and `LMA pause` / `LMA start` commands are unaffected. The same changes can be asked for out loud: "Hey Alex, switch to translator mode", "go back to assistant mode", "listen mode", "always listen" or "wake mode" — the assistant confirms in one sentence ("Switching to translator mode.") and then switches, with the same rules and restore as the chat command — and a translator is switched back by saying "stop translating" (or "translator off", "exit translator mode" and similar; the assistant's name is optional, but the phrase has to be the whole utterance, so a sentence that merely contains it is translated as usual). The phrase is read from the participants' transcript as well as from Nova Sonic's own, and when Nova Sonic takes it for its pause phrase the translator still switches back rather than pausing; a `LMA wake` declined while translating now also says that "stop translating" does it. A spoken mode change other than "stop translating" is acted on only when the assistant was addressed by wake phrase, and is logged as `route=voice`. **The assistant pauses while it switches** — reckon on about a second — because its mode decides its system prompt and tool set, both of which are fixed for the life of a model session, so LMA rebuilds the session around the new mode. **The transcript is not interrupted**: it is cut from a separate audio path that the switch does not touch. **`VoiceAssistantActivationMode` is now a default rather than a fixed setting**, and `LMA always` — or `LMA translate` and `LMA listen`, which both need an always-listening assistant — will override it for one meeting, on the word of any participant including a guest. That changes when meeting audio is streamed to the model and what the meeting costs, so it is worth knowing if you chose `wake_phrase` for either reason. A switch is **not** written back to configuration: the configuration page keeps holding the default and the next meeting starts in it. There is no per-deployment switch to turn these commands off; say so on the issue tracker if you need one. Nova Sonic only; on ElevenLabs the command is answered and declined. No new stack parameters and no new configuration fields. See [Changing Mode During a Meeting](docs/voice-assistant.md#changing-mode-during-a-meeting) for the vocabulary in full, what a switch does to a conversation in progress, how to read the `[assistant-toggle]` log line, and one caveat on the optional MicroVM ASR engine.

- **`LMA help` in the meeting chat lists the Virtual Participant's chat commands.** Typing `LMA help` (or `@LMA help`, `help LMA`) makes the Virtual Participant post the commands it answers: `LMA leave`, `LMA pause` / `LMA start` and `LMA help`, plus `LMA ask` when a Strands meeting assistant is configured, plus the voice-assistant mode commands `LMA translate`, `LMA assist`, `LMA listen`, `LMA always` and `LMA wake` when a Nova Sonic voice assistant is configured. Each command is listed once, in its canonical form, and the list is generated from the same vocabulary the commands are matched against, so the two cannot drift apart. It is posted as one or two single-line chat messages on every platform. See [In-meeting Chat Commands](docs/virtual-participant.md#in-meeting-chat-commands).

- **`lma vp create --as-user <email>` launches a Virtual Participant for an LMA user.** A VP launched from the CLI or SDK previously belonged to no LMA user: it started with a fresh browser profile, joined Zoom as a guest, and so stopped at Zoom's sign-in prompt for meetings that admit only signed-in users. With `--as-user` (SDK: `client.vp.create(as_user=...)`) the VP is owned by that user and appears in their Virtual Participants list, restores their saved browser profile, and for Zoom signs in with their stored Zoom credentials when they have saved some (`--zoom-credentials` / `--no-zoom-credentials` to require or skip that). The caller needs `cognito-idp:AdminGetUser` and `cognito-idp:ListUsers` on the LMA user pool, and `secretsmanager:DescribeSecret` on the stack's Zoom credentials secrets. See [Launching a VP for an LMA user](docs/lma-cli.md#launching-a-vp-for-an-lma-user---as-user).

- **⚠️ The pause and resume chat commands now have to be addressed to LMA: type `LMA pause` and `LMA start`.** A bare `PAUSE` or `START` in the meeting chat no longer pauses or resumes the transcript; it is treated as ordinary chat, the same as a bare `leave` or `translate`. Every Virtual Participant chat command now names `LMA`: `@LMA pause` and `pause LMA` work too, matched case-insensitively with punctuation ignored. If participants in your meetings type `PAUSE` / `START`, tell them to type `LMA pause` / `LMA start` instead, and update any customised `StartRecordingMessage`, `StopRecordingMessage` or `IntroMessage` text that tells people to type the bare word. A customised `IntroMessage` is used as it is. See [In-meeting Chat Commands](docs/virtual-participant.md#in-meeting-chat-commands).

- **The meeting-assist stack's IAM roles are covered by the deployment's permissions boundary.** `PermissionsBoundaryArn` is now passed into the `MEETINGASSISTSETUP` nested stack, as it already is into the AI, Cognito, Bedrock Knowledge Base, ASR MicroVM, WebSocket transcriber and Virtual Participant stacks, so every IAM role it creates carries the boundary policy you named. **⚠️ If you deploy with a permissions boundary, confirm it permits what those roles need before updating** — the meeting-assist function's Amazon Bedrock model invocation, its DynamoDB and Secrets Manager reads, its `appsync:GraphQL` writes and its KMS use, plus the setup function's CloudFormation, Lambda, IAM, S3 and CloudFront calls. A boundary that does not allow them will fail the stack update. The new troubleshooting agent's role is also covered, and its failure mode is different: a boundary that omits what it needs — CloudWatch Logs `DescribeLogGroups`/`StartQuery`/`GetQueryResults`/`StopQuery` plus `CreateLogStream`/`PutLogEvents` on its own log group, `cloudformation:DescribeStacks`/`ListStackResources`, `dynamodb:GetItem`, `kms:Decrypt`/`DescribeKey` on the deployment's key, Amazon Bedrock model invocation, `appsync:GraphQL` and `xray:Put*` — lets the stack update succeed and makes the agent fail at run time instead. Leaving out the log writes is the hardest version of that to spot: the agent runs and records nothing. Deployments that leave `PermissionsBoundaryArn` empty, which is the default, are unaffected.

- **The voice assistant now goes quiet silently in `group` mode, instead of sounding a tone.** Asking a `group`-mode assistant to stop used to play a short descending tone as it muted itself; it now simply stops, which is what `normal` mode does and what it does whichever of the stop mechanisms acts first. A tone is still used in translator mode, where the assistant speaks no acknowledgement and so has no other way to confirm that it has paused. **If your meetings relied on hearing the tone, there is no longer an audible confirmation in `group` mode** — the assistant often says "Okay" as it goes, but that is best-effort and may be absent, so the assistant falling silent is the signal to listen for. See [Asking the Assistant to Stop Talking](docs/voice-assistant.md#asking-the-assistant-to-stop-talking).

- **Changing a deployment's shared configuration is an Admin operation, and every change records who made it.** The chat buttons, the Nova Sonic voice assistant settings, the transcript summary prompts and the Transcription Engine selection are account-wide: one stored record drives every meeting in the deployment. Saving any of them now requires membership of the **Admin** Cognito group, pinned on the AppSync field and re-checked in the resolver behind it, so the two are independent. **If someone needs to change these settings, add them to the Admin group** on **Configuration ▸ Users**. Three pages say so themselves: **Configuration ▸ Nova Sonic Config**, **Transcript Summary Prompts** and **Transcription Engine** now show a user outside the group a short message naming the setting and suggesting they ask an administrator, in place of the form. (The **Edit Chat Buttons** dialog on the meeting page is unchanged for users: it is opened from a menu only an Admin sees.) Reading is unchanged wherever the product depends on it: the Meeting Assist chat panel still loads its quick-action buttons for whoever is viewing a meeting, and the Stream Audio and Upload Audio forms still read which transcription engine is selected. Reading the Nova Sonic and summary-prompt records from the web app is restricted to the same group, matching the admin pages that display them; both keep their IAM authorization mode, so a service reading them with SigV4 is unaffected. Each saved record now also carries `UpdatedBy` — the saver's email address, or their Cognito subject where the token carries no email — and `UpdatedAt` in ISO-8601 UTC; both are written from the verified identity and a value sent by a client is ignored. No parameters change and no migration is required.

- **Third-party integration credentials are held in AWS Secrets Manager.** The Tavily, ElevenLabs and Simli API keys, the Zoom Meeting SDK client secret and the Azure Communication Services connection string are each kept in a secret the stack creates, encrypted with the deployment's customer-managed KMS key, and redacted from the Virtual Participant's logs. **You enter each credential in the same parameter as before, and a stack update moves it into its secret.** A rotated Virtual Participant credential reaches the next meeting to start; the Tavily key takes effect as the meeting assistant's execution environments recycle. Each credential also accepts the ARN of a secret you manage yourself. ⚠️ **Rotate each credential you have configured once, so the value in use is one stored this way, and check that `CloudWatchLogsExpirationInDays` — which sets how long the Virtual Participant's logs are kept — is the retention you want.** See [Integration Credentials](docs/infrastructure-and-security.md#rotating-a-credential) for each credential's rotation procedure, and for the 30-day name-recovery window when clearing and re-setting one.

- **Meeting summaries now use Claude Sonnet 5.5 by default, and are no longer cut short.** The summary request previously allowed the model only 512 output tokens, which was enough for most meetings but not all — a long meeting's summary could stop mid-sentence, with nothing to indicate it had been truncated. The budget is now large enough for a full summary, a truncated reply is logged, and a reply with no text at all is reported as a failure instead of overwriting a good summary with a blank one. The default summarisation model moves to `global.anthropic.claude-sonnet-5-5`, which writes a fuller summary; expect roughly 27% more input tokens and two to three times the output tokens per meeting than before, and set `BedrockModelId` back to `global.anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0` if you prefer the previous cost and length. The Meeting Assistant's default is deliberately unchanged, because it answers during the meeting and the newer models pause to reason before replying. Enable access to any model you select in the Amazon Bedrock console before deploying.

- **The transcript sent for summarisation is no longer trimmed.** A word-budget cut dating from smaller context windows has been removed, along with the `TOKEN_COUNT` environment variable that configured it; it was disabled in shipped deployments, and when enabled it dropped the end of the meeting, where decisions usually land. An end-of-call hook that still sends `TokenCount` in its event is accepted and the field ignored.

- **A summary prompt is read from the prompt-template record only when its key has the documented `N#Title` form.** `N` is the sequence number that orders the sections of the summary, and it is the form the Transcript Summary page writes and the form the record's own `*Information*` text has always described. A key in any other form is no longer used as a prompt, and the summary function logs one line naming it. **Action required only if you hand-edited `CustomSummaryPromptTemplates` in DynamoDB**: a key you added without a sequence number — `MyNotes` rather than `4#MyNotes` — used to produce a section of the summary titled with the whole key, and will now be skipped. Rename it with a sequence number to get the section back. Templates created through the UI are unaffected, because it has only ever written the `N#Title` form.

- **Claude Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 can now be selected for summaries and for the Meeting Assistant.** Each is offered in both its `global.` and its `us.` inference-profile form, on both **`BedrockModelId`** (summaries, pasted meeting invitations, speaker naming) and **`MeetingAssistServiceBedrockModelID`** (the Meeting Assistant agent). The default is unchanged — both still default to Claude Haiku 4.5 — so nothing moves unless you select one. **Read what it costs before you do:** these models reason before they answer and the reasoning is billed as output, so one summary of the same meeting uses roughly three to four times the output tokens it does on Claude Haiku 4.5, and their tokenizer counts the same transcript as about a quarter more input tokens. A per-meeting bill several times larger is the expected outcome; the better summary is what you are paying for. You must also enable access to the model you pick in the Amazon Bedrock console for your region first. See [Bedrock model choices](docs/cloudformation-parameters.md#bedrock-model-choices). The Meeting Assistant's knowledge-base tools are not affected by this choice — they generate with the model named by `MeetingAssistKnowledgeBaseModelID`, described next.

- **The Meeting Assistant's knowledge bases now have a Bedrock model parameter of their own — set it before updating if you changed the assistant's model.** The new **`MeetingAssistKnowledgeBaseModelID`** names the model the assistant's two knowledge-base tools — `document_search` over your documents and `meeting_history` over past meeting transcripts — generate their answers with. The agent itself still runs on `MeetingAssistServiceBedrockModelID`. Until now there was one parameter for both jobs, and these tools generated with whatever the agent was set to. ⚠️ **If you left `MeetingAssistServiceBedrockModelID` at its default, nothing moves and there is nothing to do. If you changed it, do two things before you update:** set `MeetingAssistKnowledgeBaseModelID` to the closest offered match for your current agent model, and make sure access to that model is enabled in the Amazon Bedrock console for your region. The deployment's parameter validation now converses with this model as well as the agent's, so a stack that has never had access to the new parameter's value is rejected at parameter validation rather than part-way through — unless you set `ModelValidation` to `false`, in which case nothing is checked up front and a missing access grant surfaces as a failing tool during a meeting. What the change is worth depends on which model you were running: on Claude Sonnet 4.5, Sonnet 4.6 or Opus 4.6 your knowledge bases were generating on that model and will move to the new parameter's value, so name the same id to keep what you had; on Claude Opus 4.1 both tools were being refused on every call, and this release is what makes them work; on a Nova model or Claude Opus 4 their behaviour was never established, and the new parameter puts them on a model that has been. The Claude 5.5 models become selectable for the agent in this same release, so no deployment is upgrading off one: they are an agent choice only, and retrieval always uses the new parameter. The two parameters are separate because a Bedrock Knowledge Base generates through orchestration and generation prompt templates that it supplies, and so accepts fewer models than the agent's own API does. Which models those are turns out to be a property of the individual model rather than of its family or version — Claude Opus 4.1 is refused where Claude Opus 4.6 is accepted — so the new parameter offers only the seven models verified against a live knowledge base, and defaults to `global.anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0`. The point of the split is that the agent's model becomes a free choice — including the Claude 5.5 generation, which is not offered for retrieval — with retrieval unaffected. Should a knowledge base ever be given a model it cannot generate with, the tool it backs is not offered to the agent and the function's log names both the model and the parameter to change. See [The knowledge-base model](docs/meeting-assistant.md#the-knowledge-base-model) and the [v0.3.11 migration notes](docs/stack-updates-and-upgrades.md#v0311).

### Fixed

- **A paused Nova Sonic translator no longer resumes by itself, and answers to the wake phrase.** Paused with its pause phrase ("translator mute"), the translator was still sent the meeting's audio: it kept replying to what it heard ("I'm paused, say translator unmute") with speech the pause dropped, and it could resume translating on speech that only mentioned the translator. A participant who said "Hey Alex, …" to a paused translator was never answered, because only the resume phrase was acted on. Now, in a meeting with a participants' transcript (Amazon Transcribe or the MicroVM ASR engine), a paused translator is not sent the meeting's audio and is resumed from that transcript by its resume phrase; the wake phrase switches it to assistant mode and what was asked is answered there; and "stop translating" switches it to assistant mode, as it does while translating. Without a participants' transcript a paused translator behaves as before. If the transcript stops partway through a meeting while the translator is paused, `LMA assist` in the chat is the way out. See [Changing Mode During a Meeting](docs/voice-assistant.md#changing-mode-during-a-meeting).

- **The Virtual Participant live view no longer flickers and reconnects every second.** Once the viewer had reconnected for any reason — most often a `MANUAL_ACTION_REQUIRED` prompt, which switches View Only off, or toggling View Only or Scale to Fit by hand — it could start tearing down and reopening its connection about once a second, minting a new token each time, which made the screen flash and typing into the Virtual Participant (for example a Zoom one-time passcode) difficult; on one MicroVM meeting the viewer reconnected 114 times in under two and a half minutes. The cause was that the viewer took the delayed disconnect event of the connection it had just closed itself for a dropped connection and scheduled another reconnect, so each reconnect caused the next. The viewer now holds one connection per session: Virtual Participant status updates and the View Only and Scale to Fit toggles apply to the open connection without reconnecting, a connection the viewer closed itself is never retried, and a connection that drops is retried with a delay that keeps growing (1 second, doubling to at most 15) until a connection has held for 30 seconds. After eight drops in a row without such a connection, the viewer stops retrying and offers Reconnect; previously every brief connection started the count over, so it never stopped. The viewer is the same on every `VPLaunchType`, so the ECS launch types get the same change.

- **Publishing no longer leaves the checkout modified.** `publish.sh` and `lma deploy --from-code` wrote a fresh `source_hash` into the tracked templates of the LLM prompt template, chat button and Nova Sonic configuration stacks on every run, so each publish left `git status` dirty and changed the input of the next publish's change detection. The hash is now written into the packaged copy that is uploaded, and the tracked templates are left alone. A change to a stack's `source/` still changes the deployed hash, so the seeding custom resource still re-runs when its content changes.

- **`make docker-build-check-transcriber`, `make docker-build-check-vp` and `make docker-build-check-all` now fail when an image build fails.** A shell precedence slip made each target report success whatever `docker build` returned. Only removing the throwaway image tag afterwards may now fail. The targets also check that the Docker daemon is reachable, not only that the `docker` CLI is installed, and stop with an error when it is not.

- **`utilities/websocket-client` builds again, and CI now type-checks it.** Its source imported a transcriber module path that does not exist in this repository, so `npm run build` failed with `TS2307`. The client now declares the START/END control frame it sends, and `make lint-typescript` (part of the `Lint and unit tests` check) runs its `tsc` and eslint.

- **A passing network error during the WebSocket transcriber's image build no longer fails the deployment.** The `docker build` step of the in-stack CodeBuild project that builds the transcriber container pulls its base images from Amazon ECR Public and its packages from apt and npm, and a single timed-out or reset connection to any of them failed the build, which failed the stack update and rolled it back; on one nightly run, a TLS handshake timeout fetching the Node.js base image also left the stack in `UPDATE_ROLLBACK_FAILED`, because the rollback reached the Virtual Participant MicroVM image while its update's build was still running. That `docker build` step is now retried, up to three attempts, when it ends on a network error; any other failure of it still fails at once. The project's other commands (its `npm install` and the push to Amazon ECR) are not retried. To leave room for the retries, the project's build timeout is now 20 minutes rather than 10, so a build that hangs takes up to 20 minutes to fail.

- **A muted voice assistant no longer runs lookups nobody asked for.** In `group` mode — and in `normal` mode after being asked to stop talking — the assistant kept answering the meeting conversation it heard while muted, and asked for meeting-assistant lookups as part of those answers: on one recorded always-listening meeting, 31 Strands agent invocations in four minutes of ordinary team discussion, each costing a Lambda invocation and Bedrock usage, with every answer then dropped by the mute. The cause was that muting only gated the assistant's audio, not its tool calls. A lookup requested while the assistant is muted is now answered without invoking the Strands Lambda unless the turn that prompted it addressed the assistant by wake phrase, on both Nova Sonic and ElevenLabs; addressing the assistant works as before. On Nova Sonic the group-mode prompt also asks the model to look things up only when someone asks it to. See [Group Meeting Mode](docs/voice-assistant.md#group-meeting-mode).

- **A Virtual Participant's transcript no longer gives the last words of one person's sentence to the next person who speaks.** On Amazon Transcribe a sentence such as "Make sure your change actually got merged in." could end in a separate one-word line, "in.", under the name of whoever spoke next. A change of active speaker was placed at the last word Transcribe had returned when the change was seen, most of a second before the change; it is now placed on the transcription audio clock when the meeting reports it, and each word is named after the attendee the meeting showed during that word. With voice separation off, each row also starts at its own first word, where it used to take the time at which that speaker had been noticed; and after Transcribe reconnects, rows continue from the end of all the audio the previous session was sent rather than from its last word, so they sit later by that session's trailing silence, closer to real meeting time. Separately, on the Zoom web client the on-demand MicroVM engine moved every change 2 seconds earlier to allow for a reporting delay that only the Zoom Meeting SDK join has; the web client now gets no allowance on either engine, and the SDK join gets 2 seconds. See [How Speaker Names Are Assigned](docs/virtual-participant.md#how-speaker-names-are-assigned).

- **A Virtual Participant's transcript no longer merges a participant's speech and the voice assistant's into one line.** On Amazon Transcribe, a participant who spoke while the assistant was answering ended up in the same transcript line as the assistant — for example "What's the weather today in search for the current weather in Herndon for you." — and a participant's "Hey Alex, stop" spoken over the assistant could be lost inside the assistant's line and never acted on. The Virtual Participant sent Transcribe a single mono mix of the meeting and the assistant, so both shared one channel. With a voice assistant configured it now sends one stream with two channels: participants on `CALLER`, named from the meeting roster and the only lines checked for wake phrases and stop requests, and the assistant on `AGENT` under the Virtual Participant's name, as the MicroVM ASR engine and Stream Audio already do. A two-channel stream is billed for its duration like a mono one; without a voice assistant the stream is unchanged, and the audio and video recordings still contain both. See [How the Assistant Appears in the Transcript](docs/voice-assistant.md#how-the-assistant-appears-in-the-transcript). (#840)
- **The Nova Sonic translator keeps translating, instead of drifting into repeating what was said.** Participants could hear what they had just said read back to them in their own language — now and then on the first turn of a session, and more and more often as a meeting went on. The translator prompt said "the other language", which left the choice to the model; it now names the target language for each direction. And each translated turn now gets a new Nova Sonic session, because within one session the model drifts: it starts transcribing English speech as Spanish and "translating" that back into English. When a session starts speaking a translation, the meeting's audio moves to a new session, with the audio heard since the speaker finished replayed into it so the next speaker's first words are not lost; the old session finishes its translation and is closed. A participant who starts speaking while a translation is playing no longer cuts it off, and the next translation plays after it. Replaying synthesized meetings against Nova 2 Sonic, one session translated 25 of 40 utterances into the right language and a session per turn translated 64 of 64; this has not yet been verified in a live meeting. Each turn writes a `[translator-turn]` line to the Virtual Participant log. A translator meeting therefore holds more than one Nova Sonic stream at a time (usually two, sometimes three) and opens one per turn, which counts against the account's Bedrock quota of 20 concurrent Nova Sonic streams per region, and it adds a little billed speech input per turn — see *What this costs* under [Translator Mode](docs/voice-assistant.md#translator-mode). When Bedrock refuses the next session for that quota, the translator keeps translating on the session it already has, writes `[translator-turn] action=kept-session reason=throttled` to the log, and tries a new session again on a later turn after a backoff. (#841, #870, #878)
- **A Virtual Participant in translator mode now keeps a continuous transcript.** The assistant's translations never appeared in the transcript, and a participant's sentence the assistant began translating stayed marked as in progress for the rest of the meeting, because everything Amazon Transcribe heard while the assistant was speaking — participants included — was kept out of the transcript. Participants are now transcribed in full on a channel of their own, and each translation is written as the assistant's line from the voice assistant's own text, so the transcript no longer depends on the meeting's Transcribe language matching the language the assistant speaks. A translation line is the text Nova Sonic generated, so after an interruption it can include words that were not spoken. In every meeting mode, a sentence still in progress when Amazon Transcribe ends its session and the Virtual Participant reconnects is now completed from its last partial instead of being left in progress. The translation lines are written for Amazon Nova Sonic only; the same text is also used on the MicroVM ASR engine whenever no assistant channel is transcribing the assistant's voice. (#828, #840)

- **A wake-phrase voice assistant no longer goes back to sleep in the middle of an answer.** The activation window ran from the wake phrase and only waited for the assistant while its speech was still arriving from the model — but Nova Sonic delivers speech several times faster than it is played, so with a lookup taking 5-10 seconds the session regularly closed while the answer was still being heard. A participant who then said "Hey Alex, stop" was talking to a session that could no longer hear them, so the rest of the answer played out regardless, and a correction spoken over an answer could be cut off the same way. `VoiceAssistantActivationDuration` is now idle time: the window does not lapse while a lookup is running or while the assistant can still be heard, and it starts again when the assistant is addressed again or spoken over. An idle assistant still closes its session after the configured duration of quiet; conversation among other participants can delay that by a few seconds at a time, never beyond twice the duration, so a session is held open longer only while the assistant is actually in use. See [wake_phrase](docs/voice-assistant.md#wake_phrase). (#831)
- **A wake-phrase ElevenLabs voice assistant no longer disconnects in the middle of an answer or just after a lookup.** With ElevenLabs as the provider, the assistant could go quiet partway through answering, or straight after a Strands agent lookup returned and before it had said anything, and a follow-up question or a correction spoken over it inside the window could be cut off the same way. The activation window was still a fixed timer from the wake phrase that only waited while audio was actually playing, so the gap between a lookup finishing and the answer starting, or a pause between audio chunks, closed the connection. ElevenLabs now uses the same idle-time window as Nova Sonic: it does not lapse while a lookup is running (including several at once) or while the agent's audio is arriving, queued or still heard in the meeting, and it restarts when the assistant is addressed by wake phrase again or spoken over. An idle assistant still disconnects after `VoiceAssistantActivationDuration`. An audio chunk that arrives just as playback of the previous ones finishes is also played straight away, rather than held back until the next answer starts. See [wake_phrase](docs/voice-assistant.md#wake_phrase). (#861)
- **The voice assistant now stays awake for the configured activation duration.** After a wake phrase, the assistant always went back to sleep after 30 seconds whatever `VoiceAssistantActivationDuration` was set to: the transcript path that activates it on a wake phrase asked for 30 seconds explicitly, which overrode the configured value for both Nova Sonic and ElevenLabs, and Nova Sonic was not given the configured value at all, so in group meeting mode it also re-muted itself 30 seconds after it was last addressed. Both providers now use the configured duration; stacks left at the default of 30 seconds see no change. (#821, #831)

- **The Amazon Nova Sonic voice assistant acknowledges every lookup briefly, and no longer speaks its plan aloud.** Asked a question that needed a search, the assistant could say something like "The user asked about the weather today... I should first acknowledge their request... Then, I'll call the strands_ agent" into the meeting instead of a short "Let me search for that information." The built-in tool instructions were a numbered step-by-step procedure that the model recited back, and Nova Sonic has no private reasoning channel, so anything it plans is spoken; the instructions are now a single preamble sentence, required before every lookup including follow-up questions, plus an explicit rule never to voice reasoning, plans or tool names. These instructions are added by the Virtual Participant itself, so they apply with a custom Nova Sonic system prompt too. The model still skips the sentence sometimes — on follow-up questions in particular, which left the meeting in silence for the whole 5-10 second lookup — so if it has not started speaking within a second of a lookup starting, the Virtual Participant plays a short "One moment, let me look that up." of its own, spoken by Nova Sonic in the configured voice and rendered once at startup. It is never played while the assistant is muted or being asked to stop, or once someone has spoken since the lookup started, and a stop request or barge-in cuts it off; each lookup writes a `[tool-ack]` line to the Virtual Participant's log naming which acknowledgement played and how long after the lookup started. Rendering the clip opens one short Nova Sonic session when each Virtual Participant starts, in the background, so it never delays the start; a render refused for the account's Nova Sonic quota is retried over about half a minute and then skipped, leaving that meeting without the fallback acknowledgement. See [Pre-Tool Acknowledgment](docs/nova-sonic-setup.md#pre-tool-acknowledgment). (#827, #831)

- **The Amazon Nova Sonic voice assistant now speaks the whole answer to a lookup.** Asked for something with a long, formatted answer — a five-day forecast, say — the assistant could say only its first line ("here's the 5-day forecast for Herndon, VA:") and then fall silent until its activation window ran out. The meeting assistant writes its answers in markdown for the meeting chat, and the voice assistant passed that markdown to Nova Sonic unchanged; replaying a live meeting's lookups, Nova Sonic ended its turn after the first line of a markdown answer every time, and spoke the same answer in full every time once it was plain sentences. The voice assistant now reduces the answer to plain spoken text first — headings, list items and table rows become sentences, and links and emphasis keep only their words. An ElevenLabs voice assistant is handed its lookup answers in the same plain form. Answers in the meeting assistant's own chat are unchanged. (#862, #883)

- **An always-listening Nova Sonic voice assistant no longer opens another session as it stops.** Stopping the assistant closes its Nova Sonic session, and the stream ending during that close was taken for an unexpected disconnect, so a replacement session was opened after the agent had stopped. (#870)

- **A Virtual Participant no longer pauses or resumes transcription on a chat message that only mentions `PAUSE` or `START`.** On Zoom, Teams and Webex, any message containing the uppercase word — *"PAUSED"*, *"please don't PAUSE"*, *"START time is 3pm"* — paused or resumed the transcript, because those platforms searched the message for the word rather than comparing the whole message. The two commands now follow the same rule as `LMA leave` and the assistant-mode commands on every platform: the message must be the addressed command and nothing else, in any case (`LMA pause`, `@LMA start`) — see the entry under Changed on the `LMA` addressee. See [In-meeting Chat Commands](docs/virtual-participant.md#in-meeting-chat-commands).

- **A voice-assistant search that finishes after the model stream dropped no longer answers into the wrong session.** A meeting-assistant search takes five to ten seconds. If the model stream closed or errored during one — which an always-listening assistant recovers from by opening a replacement session — the result was handed to that replacement, identified by a reference it had never issued. A result now reaches only the session that asked for it, and is otherwise discarded with a log line, so the assistant stays silent on that question instead of answering unpredictably. Ask again to get an answer. (The four-minute keep-alive refresh was never affected: it already waits for a search to finish before replacing the session.)

- **"Email summary" no longer cuts the email short.** The summary was spliced into the `mailto:` link with only its line breaks and bold markers converted, so the first `#` or `&` anywhere in the text ended the body at that character and a summary that mentioned "cost & risk" arrived as a fragment. The body and the subject are both URL-encoded now, and the whole summary reaches the mail client. Nothing about how the summary is stored or displayed changes. (#779)

- **A Virtual Participant ended while it was still starting up no longer keeps showing a start-up status.** Ending a VP records `ENDED` and stops it, but a progress update the VP had already sent — "Launching browser", say — could land a moment later and leave the ended VP showing that step for good. A progress status no longer lands once the end has been recorded, and a VP that has been told to stop writes only its final status, so an ended VP always shows an ended or completed status. (#818)

- **A Virtual Participant that recovers from a problem no longer still shows the problem.** The status line under a VP's status is a general detail channel — the reason a meeting ended, the current step while it is still joining, the error when it fails — and the mutation that writes it leaves out fields it was not given, so nothing ever took a message off the row. A VP that hit a snag, retried and then joined kept displaying the snag for the rest of the meeting, and a `COMPLETED` meeting could still show a join sub-step. Reaching `JOINED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` with nothing new to say now clears it; a status that carries its own detail, including every join sub-step and the reason a meeting ended, is unaffected.

- **A resolved "manual action required" prompt now clears, whenever it was raised.** The prompt's own fields were never removed from the VP record, and the alert banner at the top of the UI only went away once the VP reached `JOINED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` — so a sign-in or CAPTCHA solved before the VP was admitted left the banner up until it joined, or for good if the meeting never started. The prompt is now removed when it is resolved, the banner clears on any status that is not `MANUAL_ACTION_REQUIRED`, and the VP returns to what it was doing when the prompt arrived. A join progress update can also no longer overwrite a prompt that is still waiting on you.

- **An unanswered "manual action required" prompt no longer chimes repeatedly.** A prompt waits on a person, often for minutes, and the Virtual Participant keeps working while it does — so each of those background updates re-announced the same prompt, re-rendering the alert and sounding the desktop notification again. An alert is now raised once per prompt: a different prompt, or the same one raised again after a timeout, still announces itself.

- **A Virtual Participant's alerts now name the meeting they are about.** The "action required" and "virtual participant failed" alerts at the top of the UI read *"— null"* where the meeting name belongs, and an action-required alert that arrived while you were on another page could show generic wording in place of the specific thing being asked of you. Both come from the same cause: these alerts are built from the live update that triggered them, and those updates did not carry the meeting name, platform or prompt detail — a subscription delivers only the fields the write behind it asked for. Every write from the Virtual Participant container now carries the full set the UI subscribes to, so each alert names its meeting and says what it needs.

- **A Virtual Participant's reported status no longer moves backwards.** Several writes carried a status they had no business setting: linking a VP to its call wrote `SCHEDULED`, a second link when transcription started wrote back whatever it had just read (defaulting to `INITIALIZING`), and clearing a manual-action prompt wrote `JOINING` — all regardless of where the VP actually was. A VP recording a meeting could therefore be shown as scheduled, as initialising, or as still joining; the second of those raced the write that reports recording has begun, seconds apart. Each write now sets only what it is responsible for, the two CallId links share one implementation, and clearing a prompt restores the status the prompt interrupted. The status is also no longer required by the update mutation, which is what lets a caller update one field on its own. Separately, the three places outside the container that write a status — the launch state machine's failure and capacity-wait steps, and the container's own start-up write — now leave a VP alone once it has reached a terminal status, the condition the task reaper already used, so a cancelled or finished VP keeps its outcome.

- **A Virtual Participant that could not get into a Webex or Chime meeting now says so.** A VP that Webex never admitted, and one that could not find Chime's Join button, both ended as `COMPLETED` — reported as a meeting that ran, with no transcript behind it — because those branches returned a code meaning "ended for a reason we could not classify", which is scored as having joined. A VP that Chime never admitted did end as `FAILED`, but gave the reason as a wrong meeting password or denied permission, neither of which that branch had any way of knowing. All three now report not having joined, as the Zoom and Teams handlers already did, so the meeting ends as `FAILED` with *"Could not join the meeting (was not admitted, or stuck on the join screen)."*

- **`lma deploy` now reports whether the stack actually changed, and can insist that it did.** CloudFormation refuses an update that would change nothing, and that was reported as a successful deployment — correct for redeploying an unchanged working copy, but indistinguishable from a real update for a pipeline that deploys in order to test what it just built. The result now carries the outcome separately, and the new `--fail-if-unchanged` flag exits non-zero when nothing was applied. The nightly integration pipeline passes it, so a stack that has stopped tracking its branch reports red instead of testing older code under a newer commit's name.

- **Integration tests for features that are off by default no longer fail the nightly run.** The nightly run forbids skipped tests, so that a lapsed credential cannot report green having exercised nothing. A test for an opt-in stack feature has nothing to exercise until somebody enables it, which is a decision about that stack's cost and deploy time rather than something a new test should force, so such tests now carry an `optional_feature` marker and skip cleanly. The voice-enrollment lifecycle test is marked accordingly; enable `EnableVoiceEnrollment` (and `EnableMicrovmAsr`, which computes the voiceprints) on a stack to exercise it there.

- **A long meeting's summary is no longer cut off part-way through.** The output budget sent to Amazon Bedrock for each summary prompt was 512 tokens, and the model wanted up to 577 for the longest meetings measured, so the last sentence or two of those summaries was simply missing — with nothing on the meeting page to say so. The budget is now 4,096 tokens for summaries, for parsing a pasted meeting invitation, and for the speaker-naming pass. This costs nothing on a normal meeting: the budget is a ceiling rather than a reservation, so a summary that takes 300 tokens is still billed for 300. A summary that does reach the ceiling is now recorded as truncated in the function's log, and a model response carrying no summary text at all is reported as an error rather than stored, so a blank summary cannot overwrite a good one. Regenerate the summary on any meeting whose summary ends mid-sentence to get the complete one.

- **Summaries, invitation parsing and speaker naming now work on reasoning-capable Bedrock models.** These three prompts sent an inference configuration and read the model's reply in a shape that only the Nova models and Claude 4.x and earlier produce. A reasoning-capable model — anything in the Claude 5 generation or later — rejects a request carrying a `temperature` setting and may answer with a reasoning block ahead of the text, so each of the three would have failed, or stored the model's internal reasoning in place of its answer, on a stack configured with one. The temperature is now sent only to models that accept one, so the determinism the summary and speaker-naming prompts rely on is unchanged on the models that have always provided it, and the reply is read by block type rather than by position, so reasoning never reaches a stored summary or the speaker-name parser. The same applies to the Meeting Assistant's agent and its direct-Bedrock fallback, and to the Virtual Participant's self-healing selector resolver. No parameters change and no deployment needs to act.

- **The Meeting Assistant now reads all of a long meeting's transcript, not just the start of it.** The assistant reads the current meeting's segments from DynamoDB itself, and that read took only the first page of results. Because the filter that excludes in-progress segments is applied after the 1 MB page limit, a meeting of any length spanned several pages — so on a long meeting the assistant summarised, extracted action items from, and answered questions about the opening stretch of the meeting as though it were the whole thing, with nothing to say it was partial. This is the same correction made in 0.3.10 to the Lambda that assembles a transcript for summaries and the knowledge base; the assistant's own copy of the read was separate and kept the single page. All pages are now read, bounded by a page limit and a check that the continuation key is advancing. Short meetings, which fit in one page, are unaffected, and answers about long meetings will be fuller — and cost more input tokens — than before. (#791)

- **The Meeting Assistant's streamed answers arrive faster, by a margin that grows with the length of the answer.** Every token of a streamed answer was published to AppSync over a connection of its own, so the assistant spent a fresh TLS handshake on each one — measured from the deployed function's logs at 0.107 seconds per token, which on a typical answer is of the order of ten seconds of pure overhead, and which penalised exactly the models that stream most fluently. The tokens of one answer now share a single connection, opened when the answer starts and closed when it ends; a failed publish gives up that connection so the next token reconnects, which keeps one lost token from costing the rest of the answer. Separately, an answer nobody is subscribed to is no longer published token by token at all: the Virtual Participant's voice assistant calls the assistant directly and speaks the complete answer it receives, so those invocations skip the token stream and return the same answer sooner. Nothing changes for the chat panel or the wake phrase, and no parameters change. See [Chat Interface](docs/meeting-assistant.md#chat-interface).

- **`publish` now fails when the WebSocket transcriber's artifacts cannot be built.** `lma-websocket-transcriber-stack/deployment/build-s3-dist.sh` ran every step regardless of whether the previous one worked and returned the exit status of its closing `echo`, so a build step that could not start was still reported as a successful build. The only visible symptom was elsewhere and much later: the stack's packaged template never reached S3, and the deployment that needed it failed with `NoSuchKey` on a nested template, pointing at CloudFormation rather than at the build. The script now stops at the first failing step. If you have published from a working copy and seen that error, re-publish — the earlier run uploaded the transcriber's source bundle but not its template.

- **A transcript segment is no longer lost when AppSync answers the sentiment or meeting-status query with no data.** A finished transcript segment triggers a read-back of the meeting's scored segments to aggregate sentiment, and the end of a meeting reads the meeting's current status to tell a first end-of-meeting event from a redelivered one. Both of those fields are nullable in the schema, and a read that came back without one failed on an error naming neither the field asked for nor the response received — and because the sentiment read happens before the segment's own write is sent, the transcript line went with it. Both reads now log what came back and carry on, whether the read returned something unusable or failed outright: a sentiment read that cannot be completed aggregates nothing and leaves the meeting's stored sentiment alone, and an unknown status leaves the meeting treated as still open, which is what that check is there to do. A list of scored segments that comes back empty is still a real answer and still aggregates to an empty sentiment. The third read-back, for who a meeting is shared with, has no substitute for the fields it returns and still fails the stream record carrying it, now naming the field and the response shape in the log rather than neither.

- **A write the stored meeting has already superseded no longer fails its record.** Each of these writes carries a condition — create the meeting only if it does not exist, keep a partial transcript segment from overwriting the final one, apply an aggregation only if it is newer than the stored one — and a rejected condition means the row already holds the version that should win. The check for that read the error as a dictionary where the GraphQL client raises an exception, so it never matched, and the write ended on its first error instead. A rejected condition is now recognised and logged at `INFO`, and any other write error is retried as it was always meant to be — which means a write that is going to fail takes up to about ten seconds of backoff to get there, where it used to fail at once. The writes for a record run concurrently, so a failing batch is reported roughly that much later rather than once per write. A `StartOfCallLambdaHookFunctionArn` hook is invoked at most once per meeting: a start event for a meeting that is already created does not invoke it again, and an invocation is not retried, so a hook that must not be missed should not be the only record that a meeting started.

- **The log lines for a failed meeting write name the error and where it happened rather than carrying the request.** Each one gives the exception's type, the GraphQL error class and the field it happened on, and the chain of frames it was raised through. The request itself stays on the debug-level entry for the attempt being described, so raise the function's log level to `DEBUG` to see it. No action is needed beyond the usual stack update for any of these.

- **Copy and paste shortcuts work in the macOS Desktop Capture App.** ⌘C, ⌘V, ⌘X, ⌘A and ⌘Z did nothing in the sign-in form or any other field — paste worked only from the right-click menu, which got in the way of password managers — because the app had no menu bar of its own, and macOS delivers those shortcuts through the Edit menu. The app now has the standard app, Edit and Window menus, so ⌘, (Settings), ⌘W and ⌘Q work too, and quitting while recording stops the recording first, as **Stop** does. To pick this up, download the macOS package again and re-run the installer; see [Updating to a new version](docs/desktop-capture-app.md#updating-to-a-new-version). ([#756](https://github.com/aws-samples/amazon-transcribe-live-meeting-assistant/issues/756))

- **A second recording started from the desktop capture app without a meeting name no longer replaces the first one.** The macOS and Windows apps chose their default meeting ID, `LMA native prototype - <time>`, once when the app launched, so every unnamed recording in that run used the same ID: a later recording overwrote the earlier one's transcript rows and audio file, and its summary was built from a mix of the two. The default ID is now taken when each recording starts; a `--call-id` or `LMA_CALL_ID` set by the user is still used as given, and on Windows the time is written in the same ISO format whatever the system locale. To pick this up, download the macOS or Windows package again and re-run the installer; see [Updating to a new version](docs/desktop-capture-app.md#updating-to-a-new-version).

### Changed

- **Building the Lambda bundles outside SAM's build container is now possible, via `SAM_BUILD_IN_CONTAINER=false`.** The default is unchanged (`true`) and remains the right choice on a developer machine, where Docker is a prerequisite and the container is what guarantees Lambda-compatible wheels whatever the host is. Set it to `false` only on a builder that already *is* the Lambda platform — linux/amd64 on python3.12 — and has no Docker daemon available, such as a CI runner; on anything else it produces bundles Lambda cannot import, and the failure appears at runtime rather than during the build. Honoured by `lma-ai-stack/Makefile`, the transcriber's `build-s3-dist.sh`, and `lma publish`'s prerequisite check, which no longer requires a Docker daemon when the flag is set. `lma publish` also checks for the AWS CLI up front, since `cfn package` shells out to it for every nested stack.

## [0.3.10] - 2026-10-09

### Added

- **Amazon Nova Sonic can be reached in a different region from the rest of the stack.** The new **`AmazonNovaSonicRegion`** parameter names the region to invoke the Nova Sonic model in, so a deployment pinned to a region where Nova Sonic is not offered — for data-residency or compliance reasons — can still use the Nova Sonic voice assistant; leave it empty, the default, to use the stack's own region, which is what every existing deployment gets. ([#508](https://github.com/aws-samples/amazon-transcribe-live-meeting-assistant/issues/508))

### Changed

- **Meeting summaries are now generated from the transcript as spoken, rather than from one with filler words stripped out.** The pass that removed "um", "uh", "mhm" and "like" before summarisation has been deleted: it matched those letters wherever a word began with them and deleted only the prefix, so "umbrella" became "brella" and "Likewise" became "wise", and because it removed every "like", meaning-bearing uses went too. The models summarising these transcripts handle disfluent speech without help. Summaries will read slightly differently after this update and should be more faithful; stored transcripts were never altered by this step, stripping of the web UI's own markup is unchanged, and no parameters change.

- **The default on-demand speech bundle is now `parakeet-tdt-v3-titanet-small`** (Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 on the offline engine, CC-BY-4.0), the most accurate model tested live and the only one that labelled every speaker turn of 2.5 s or more correctly. On update the MicroVM image is rebuilt (about 20 minutes), and rows appear when an utterance closes, within about 20 s of speech, rather than as live partial text. To keep live text while someone speaks, set the `AsrDefaults` mapping to a `-live-` bundle or to `fastconformer-titanet-small`; switching bundles is a developer change to that mapping. See [MicroVM ASR](docs/microvm-asr.md#model-bundles).

- **Updating from 0.3.8 or earlier:** a deployment that had switched streaming meetings onto the on-demand engine selects it again on the Transcription Engine page, because the saved setting was renamed in 0.3.9.

- **Dependency updates across every stack**, landed as weekly grouped Dependabot batches — the web UI, WebSocket transcriber, Virtual Participant backend, browser extension, docs-site, the Python Lambda requirements and layers, and the boto3/botocore/boto3-stubs/aws-lambda-powertools/cfn-lint/aws-sam-cli/phonenumbers/ruff tooling family — with no build regressions. React 19 stays held back by `@azure/communication-react`'s peer range. No parameters change and no action is needed beyond the usual stack update, though the Virtual Participant and transcriber changes take effect once their container images are rebuilt.

### Fixed

- **The meetings list no longer omits meetings from a shared date range.** Where several people record meetings around the same time, a user who could see only some of the meetings in a range could page past meetings entirely, so they appeared on no page at all: the continuation marker pointed at the end of the underlying DynamoDB page rather than at the meeting the listing had stopped on, and at the default page size of 50 a single meeting belonging to someone else could hide up to 49 others. The meeting count shown alongside the list is computed separately and was always right, so it could exceed the number of rows displayed. Paging now resumes from the last meeting actually returned, and still reports the end of the listing correctly when a page fills on its final row. Administrators, who have nothing filtered out, were unaffected; no meeting data was lost, and no action is needed beyond the usual stack update.

- **A long meeting's summary, knowledge-base export and assistant answers now see the whole transcript.** The Lambda that assembles a meeting transcript read only the first page of results from DynamoDB, and because the filter that excludes in-progress (partial) segments is applied after the 1 MB read limit, even a moderately long meeting spanned several pages. Everything downstream — the automatic summary, the transcript written to the knowledge base, and every Meeting Assistant answer — described a partial meeting as though it were the whole one, with nothing to indicate it was incomplete. All pages are now read, bounded by a page limit and a check that the continuation key is advancing. Short meetings, which fit in one page, were unaffected; existing meetings are reassembled from stored segments, so a summary regenerated after the update will be complete.

- **A Teams Virtual Participant no longer leaves a meeting that is still in progress.** Teams removes the roster button — and with it the participant-count badge the VP watches — whenever the meeting toolbar collapses, most often during a content share or in a full-screen layout. That reading shared a threshold of roughly 40 seconds with the "everyone has left" check, so a routine screen share could end the VP's session mid-meeting; the exit was graceful, so the transcript and recording were saved and only the truncated recording showed that anything had happened. An unreadable count is now checked against the rest of the meeting interface and, while that is still present, does not count towards leaving — for up to half an hour at a stretch, which covers a long screen share. A meeting showing neither a readable count nor any in-meeting controls is treated as over after about five minutes, the "everyone has left" check is unchanged at about 60 seconds, and a meeting whose end Teams announces on screen is still detected on the first poll. Where Teams shows no such screen, the VP now leaves up to about four minutes later than it used to — and up to about 35 minutes later where Teams also leaves its meeting controls on screen — keeping recording until it does. Three new parameters, **`VPAttendeePollMs`**, **`VPPollsBeforeEnd`** and **`VPPollsBeforeEndMissing`**, expose the cadence and both thresholds for the Teams browser join path; defaults are unchanged behaviour and no deployment should normally need them. See [How the VP decides a meeting has ended](docs/virtual-participant.md#how-the-vp-decides-a-meeting-has-ended). ([#660](https://github.com/aws-samples/amazon-transcribe-live-meeting-assistant/issues/660))

- **The browser extension shows the platform, your name and the active speaker on Teams again.** Microsoft now serves Teams on the web from `teams.cloud.microsoft` as well as `teams.microsoft.com`, and the extension registered its Teams page reader for the original hostname only, so on the newer one the panel's **Platform Detected**, **Your name** and **Active Speaker** fields were all blank — audio capture was unaffected, which is why meetings still transcribed normally. The reader is now registered for both hostnames and for `teams.microsoft.us` for government tenants. Each reader now reports whatever it has found instead of withholding everything until it has both a name and a meeting topic, the panel keeps fields an earlier report filled in, **Your name** is pre-filled from your LMA login when the meeting page does not supply it, and a name or topic you type yourself is neither overwritten nor re-filled after you clear it. Mute detection no longer depends on winning a race against the meeting page finishing loading. To pick this up, download the extension again from the **Chrome Extension** page or the `ChromeExtensionDownloadUrl` stack output after updating and reload it in `chrome://extensions`, then reload the meeting tab — the page-reading script is only injected into tabs loaded after it. See [If the panel shows no platform, name or active speaker](docs/browser-extension.md#if-the-panel-shows-no-platform-name-or-active-speaker).

- **The browser extension recognizes Zoom on its regional hosts.** The panel named the platform only for `app.zoom.us`, so a meeting opened on a numbered regional host such as `us02web.zoom.us` showed no platform even though the page reader itself has always run on every `zoom.us` host. Zoom is now matched on any `zoom.us` hostname, and the Zoom reader reports the platform from the tab itself rather than waiting on the meeting details Zoom publishes on the page — which it no longer finds on every Zoom web client, and which it now stops polling for after two minutes instead of for the life of the tab. Where those details are missing, the meeting name and topic still have to be typed in. Platform matching is now done on the hostname rather than on a substring of the address, so a host that merely contains a platform's name is not attributed to it.

- **A Virtual Participant can join Webex meetings again.** Webex replaced the pre-join display-name field with a custom element whose input sits inside a shadow root, so the Virtual Participant waited for a field that no longer existed and the join failed with a selector timeout. Both the current and the previous markup are now recognized, and the name is replaced rather than appended, so a name persisted from an earlier session no longer produces a doubled display name such as `LMALMA (user@example.com)`. Reported and originally fixed by [@psrebniak](https://github.com/psrebniak). ([#505](https://github.com/aws-samples/amazon-transcribe-live-meeting-assistant/issues/505))

- **Webex Virtual Participants act on in-meeting chat commands again.** Webex moved its meeting chat panel onto Momentum web components, so the Virtual Participant stopped seeing chat messages and commands such as `LMA leave` were ignored. Both markups are now recognized, several messages arriving together are all read rather than only the last — including a run of messages from one person, where several share a single block — and the Webex goodbye names whoever asked the Virtual Participant to leave, as it already did on Zoom and Chime. Whether the chat panel was found is now recorded in the Virtual Participant's log. Reported and originally fixed by [@psrebniak](https://github.com/psrebniak). ([#507](https://github.com/aws-samples/amazon-transcribe-live-meeting-assistant/issues/507))

- **A transcript segment timestamped at zero seconds is no longer dropped.** Producers that put `StartTime`/`EndTime` (or `BeginOffsetMillis`/`EndOffsetMillis`) directly on the Kinesis record — the on-demand MicroVM ASR engine, and any custom producer following the same shape — had those values tested for truth rather than for presence while being normalised, so a timestamp of exactly `0` was replaced with nothing and the segment was then rejected on write, because both fields are non-nullable in the GraphQL schema. The most likely casualty was the opening segment of a meeting, which is the one that starts at zero. Timestamps are now checked for presence; `StartTime`/`EndTime` continue to take precedence over the millisecond offsets when a producer sends both. Meetings transcribed by Amazon Transcribe streaming or Transcribe Call Analytics were never affected.

- **Transcript rows containing a colon are shown in full** in the call panel and the embedded call view; text before the first colon of a caller or agent row was dropped.

- **Streaming-engine rows no longer lose a word every 20 s of pause-free speech.** The engine's forced decoder reset moves from 20 s to 60 s; on bundles with speaker labels the diarizer still closes rows at 20 s without a reset.

- **A channel that asks for no speaker labels gets none.** The microphone channel no longer carries a `(spk_0)` suffix or runs voice embedding.

- **Pausing a Virtual Participant on the on-demand engine no longer ends its transcription**, and a VP whose engine session dies mid-meeting continues on Amazon Transcribe.

- **A MicroVM the platform terminates before it starts is replaced once** before the meeting falls back to Amazon Transcribe.

- **Saving the Transcription Engine page before it finished loading reset the switches.** Save is now disabled until the form has loaded.

- **Google Meet is no longer described as a Virtual Participant platform.** The README, the quick-start guide, `docs/INDEX.md`, the Virtual Participant page and its in-app help panel all listed Google Meet alongside Zoom, Teams, Chime and WebEx, but the Virtual Participant has no Google Meet handler and fails immediately with "Unsupported meeting platform". Meet meetings are captured with the [Chrome extension](docs/browser-extension.md) instead, which is what those pages were describing, and the extension's own documentation now states which platforms it covers. Several other places said the same thing and are corrected with them, including the `start_meeting_now` MCP tool's documented platform list, the Virtual Participant column of the capture-options comparison, and two architecture diagrams. The meeting-invitation parser also auto-filled the create form from a Meet invitation, so the failure arrived only after the user had submitted every other field; it now rejects the invitation up front with a message pointing at the extension, including when the invitation carries a Meet link but no platform. Adding Virtual Participant support for Google Meet is tracked in [#661](https://github.com/aws-samples/amazon-transcribe-live-meeting-assistant/issues/661).

### Fixed

- **Removing the on-demand engine no longer fails on its image-source bucket.** The bucket is versioned and kept one zip per image rebuild, so the nested stack's delete failed with "bucket is not empty". The image-source resource now empties the bucket when the stack is deleted. See [MicroVM ASR troubleshooting](docs/microvm-asr.md#troubleshooting) for stacks deployed earlier.

- **A word at a live turn cut could appear in two consecutive transcript rows on the on-demand engine.** Word timings are reconstructed from per-token timestamps, so a one-token word starts and ends on the same timestamp and was counted both as the end of one row and the start of the next — "…run through today" followed by "today does that work". Row boundaries are now partitioned on each word's end time everywhere they are applied, including text corrections that re-read a span.

- **A short opening fragment could carry the previous speaker's label on the on-demand engine.** A leading part too brief to produce a speaker embedding inherited the label from the row before it, so the first second or two of a long utterance showed as a different speaker from the rest of it. A short leading part now merges into the part that follows and is labelled by its own embedding.

- **Live transcript rows could sort above finalized rows that ended later on the on-demand engine.** A partial carried no end time, so a live row's end stayed pinned to the moment its utterance began, while the UI orders every row by end time across both channels. Partials now carry the audio time decoded so far, and reconnect offsets track partial progress with them. Amazon Transcribe partials already carried a growing end time and were unaffected.

## [0.3.8] - 2026-09-02

### Fixed

- **Scheduled Virtual Participants never launched under `VPLaunchType=MICROVM`.** The scheduler Lambda (the DynamoDB-stream path the UI's "schedule a meeting" flow uses) had one launch path: an EventBridge schedule targeting `ecs:runTask` with `VP_LAUNCH_TYPE` forwarded straight into `LaunchType`, which under `MICROVM` failed with "launch type should be one of [EC2,FARGATE,EXTERNAL]" — and because the schedule deletes itself after firing, the VP row sat at `SCHEDULED` with no error in the UI and nothing left in the console to explain it. EventBridge Scheduler has no MicroVM target, so a `MICROVM` schedule now invokes the VP MicroVM launcher Lambda directly — the same target the "start now" state machine already uses. `EC2`/`FARGATE` are unchanged. ([#613](https://github.com/aws-samples/amazon-transcribe-live-meeting-assistant/issues/613))

- **Ending a meeting now terminates a MicroVM-hosted Virtual Participant.** The manager previously only knew how to stop ECS tasks, so a MicroVM ran until its 8-hour service ceiling — continuing to capture audio for a meeting nobody was in.

- **The Virtual Participant no longer reports `ACTIVE` when it has not actually joined** (for example when a meeting platform redirects it to a sign-in page); it now fails with a specific reason instead.

- **The Virtual Participant could not join a Teams meeting under its default display name.** Teams' pre-join name field validates against a fixed character set — letters, numbers, spaces and `- ' . _ @` — which the default identity template `LMA ({LMA_USER})` fails on the parentheses, so the join was blocked with "Your name can only include letters, numbers, spaces, and these symbols". The name is now sanitised to `LMA - user` where it is typed into the Teams form (and in the verify-and-retype comparison, which would otherwise always mismatch); chat messages, the transcript identity, every other platform and the Teams ACS SDK path keep the unmodified name.

- **The voice assistant's speech was chopped in Teams meetings** — it sounded like a square wave gating on and off 5-10 times a second, while the identical audio path was clean on Zoom. This is the open Teams audio-quality report that 0.3.7 added instrumentation for; instance size, buffers, PulseAudio and packet flow had already been ruled out by measurement, and the two remaining Teams-specific mechanisms are both fixed here. **First**, the Teams web client routes the microphone through its own noise-suppression graph and transmits *that* — recording both sides of the graph during one utterance found 0 hard cuts going in and 138 in 15 seconds coming out, 20-30 ms zero-holes at 9.2/s. Any live audio sender whose track is a `MediaStreamAudioDestinationNode` is now replaced with an unprocessed capture of the same device (the VP's "mic" is a null-sink monitor — no room, no echo, no noise to suppress), polled because Teams can swap its track back. Self-scoping by track label, so Zoom and every platform that transmits the capture track directly are no-ops by construction. **Second**, Teams negotiates Opus DTX, which makes every pause a discontinuous-transmission cycle whose exit clips the first packets of the next sentence (packet rate dipping to 10.8-32.7/s against ~50 expected at every audio onset); `usedtx=1` is now rewritten to `usedtx=0` in the remote description, costing ~14 kbps of comfort noise while idle. Known trade-off: with the raw track on the sender, the Teams client's own mute button no longer silences the agent — the VP never uses it, as agent audio is managed by the voice assistant.

- **The voice assistant could go deaf after a session refresh, and refreshed at the wrong moment in a quiet meeting.** Two defects in the `always_active` lifecycle, both observed live. The refresh gate only checked agent-side state, so the 5-minute refresh could tear down the bidirectional stream while a *user* was mid-utterance — their audio died with the old session and reached the new one only as history, which never triggers a response, so the agent appeared deaf until re-addressed and looked exactly like wake-phrase mode. User speech now stamps a last-activity time and the refresh waits for 5 seconds of quiet. Separately, Nova Sonic closes an idle stream itself at 295 s and neither meeting silence nor the 30 s keep-alive counts as interactive, so the pre-emptive refresh at 300 s lost that race in every quiet meeting, trading a planned refresh for a `ValidationException` and an unplanned one; it now runs at 240 s.

- **Zoom sign-in no longer fails on a slow page load**, and a navigation that completes just after its timeout is no longer discarded.

## [0.3.7] - 2026-08-17

### Added
- **AWS Lambda MicroVMs as the new default Virtual Participant platform** — `VPLaunchType` gains a third option, **`MICROVM`**, and now defaults to it. Each Virtual Participant runs in its own Firecracker microVM resumed from a memory+disk snapshot, using the *same container image* as the ECS launch types. The live view is ready in ~20-25 seconds, billing is per meeting rather than per warm instance, and there is no load balancer, no autoscaling and no host OS patching to manage — which also removes the class of dropped meetings caused by SSM Patch Manager rebooting an EC2 host mid-call. Under `MICROVM` the VNC ALB and its target group, listener, security groups, CloudFront `/vnc/*` behavior and Lambda@Edge auth function are not created at all (the viewer connects to each MicroVM's own HTTPS endpoint with a short-lived, port-scoped auth token), and MicroVM egress is routed through the VPC's private subnets so meeting platforms see the same NAT Elastic IP as an ECS task. `EC2` and `FARGATE` remain fully supported and unchanged — select one of them in regions without Lambda MicroVM availability.

- **Per-channel speaker identification for WebSocket streaming sessions** — the [Stream Audio](docs/stream-audio.md) tab and the [Desktop Capture App](docs/desktop-capture-app.md) can now ask Amazon Transcribe to tell apart the individual voices *within* an audio channel, enabled **independently per channel**: turn it on for the incoming/system audio when a captured call has several remote participants, for the microphone when several people share one mic (a conference room), for both, or for neither. Until now each channel got a single speaker name, so everyone on the far end of a captured call appeared as one speaker. Each distinct voice is appended to the channel's existing speaker name as `(spk_0)`, `(spk_1)`, … — e.g. `Other Participant (spk_0)` — so client-supplied names are preserved rather than replaced, and the labels flow through the live transcript (which now splits turns per voice), the transcript download, the meeting summary and the Meeting Assistant. Configured per meeting in the client's own settings — checkboxes on the Stream Audio tab, **Settings ▸ Speaker identification** in the desktop app (plus `--diarize-system` / `--diarize-mic` for CLI use) — and sent to the transcriber when the session starts. **Off by default everywhere**; the new `ShowSpeakerLabel` CloudFormation parameter sets a deployment-wide default for clients that do not send their own choice. There is no additional Amazon Transcribe charge, and the two channels are still billed as a single stream. Accuracy is best with five or fewer voices per channel; speakers are numbered per channel starting at `spk_0`, and the labels are numbers rather than names — for real participant names use the Chrome Extension or a Virtual Participant, which read them from the meeting itself.

  Transcript segments are **split where the speaker changes**, which matters because a single Amazon Transcribe result routinely spans several turns — in natural conversation there is no pause to break on, so a 30-second result containing four turns is normal. Word-level labels are noisy (a single word flips mid-utterance, and punctuation carries no label), so a run of same-speaker words must reach both `DIARIZATION_MIN_RUN_WORDS` (default 3) and `DIARIZATION_MIN_RUN_SECONDS` (default 1.0) to become its own segment; shorter runs are merged into the neighbouring turn without moving any transcript text. The defaults were fitted to a real two-speaker recording where spurious runs measured 1-2 words / 0.1-0.9 s against real turns of 6-42 words / 1.2-13.4 s. One consequence: a genuine one- or two-word interjection is attributed to the speaker around it. Every final result on a diarized channel logs a `[DIARIZATION]` line at INFO with the raw runs, the resulting segments and how many were absorbed, so the thresholds can be re-tuned from real meetings.

  An in-progress segment is additionally **bounded at 20 seconds** (`DIARIZATION_MAX_SEGMENT_SECONDS`). Amazon Transcribe caps a result near 30 seconds and never labels a partial result, so following its boundaries alone left the live transcript showing one unlabelled block for up to half a minute before it split. Each result is now chunked into windows, and a window whose audio is already in the past is emitted as final while Transcribe still considers the result partial. A window boundary can fall mid-turn, producing two adjacent segments for the same speaker, which the UI merges on display.

### Fixed
- **The macOS Desktop Capture App no longer quits itself after about an hour.** Sign-in produced a Cognito refresh token that the app then discarded, so nothing could renew the ~1 hour access token — and once it expired the transcriber rejected the WebSocket connection and the app called `exit(1)`, disappearing from the menu bar. Recovering meant quitting and relaunching, and any meeting in progress was lost. The app now keeps the refresh token and renews the session in the background before it expires (on a wall-clock schedule, so it survives the Mac sleeping), renews on demand when you start a recording after a long idle or a wake from sleep, and retries once if the server rejects a token anyway. Long meetings and a Start hours after signing in both work without interruption. If renewal genuinely cannot succeed — a changed password, an administrator sign-out, or a session older than the refresh-token lifetime — the app now **stays running** and shows "Your session expired and could not be renewed. Please sign in again." with the sign-in form, instead of vanishing. The headless CLI still exits non-zero, as before. See [issue #535](https://github.com/aws-samples/amazon-transcribe-live-meeting-assistant/issues/535).

- **The Windows Desktop Capture App no longer loses the meeting after about an hour.** Same root cause as the macOS fix above: sign-in produced a Cognito refresh token that the app discarded, so nothing could renew the ~1 hour access token, and once it expired the transcriber rejected the WebSocket connection. The Windows app never quit itself, but it ended the recording and demanded a fresh sign-in — mid-meeting, with no way to resume. It now keeps the refresh token and renews the session the same way the macOS app does: in the background before the token expires (re-checked on resume from sleep or hibernation), on demand when you start a recording after a long idle, and once more if the server rejects a token anyway. If renewal genuinely cannot succeed — a changed password, an administrator sign-out, or a session older than the refresh-token lifetime — the tray app shows "Your session expired and could not be renewed. Please sign in again." with the sign-in form; the headless CLI now exits non-zero (it previously exited 0 even on an auth failure). Like the macOS fix, the Windows client also stopped sending the (empty) `refresh_token` WebSocket parameter and now redacts tokens from connection-error logs. See [issue #535](https://github.com/aws-samples/amazon-transcribe-live-meeting-assistant/issues/535).

- **The macOS Desktop Capture App no longer announces a failed session as a finished meeting.** When a recording ended because the session could not be renewed (the path above), the app still posted "Recording stopped — your meeting is being processed", with a click-through link to a meeting that was never created, because every teardown — clean stop or fatal error — passes through the same intermediate state and the notification was decided from that state alone. The outcome is now taken from the state the session actually settles in, so a dead session reports a failure and a clean stop is unchanged. The Windows app was never affected.

- **The Virtual Participant no longer stops transcribing part-way through a meeting.** An oversized audio frame from ffmpeg was rejected by Amazon Transcribe, and that error was misclassified as a permanent configuration error — so transcription aborted for the rest of the call while the VP stayed `ACTIVE`, showing no sign of trouble in the UI (the accompanying log message also pointed at the language settings, which were not involved). Audio frames are now capped and split without losing audio, and a framing error reconnects using the existing session-resume path, which already keeps transcript timestamps on the meeting timeline.

- **Voice assistant audio quality** — the PulseAudio sinks ran at the daemon default (48 kHz stereo) while the whole audio pipeline is 16 kHz mono, so the assistant's voice was resampled three times before the meeting heard it, making it sound warbly. The daemon and all three sinks are now pinned to 16 kHz mono, and the container warns at startup if that ever drifts. Affects all launch types.

- **The Virtual Participant now leaves when a Teams meeting ends.** Teams removes the participant-count badge when the host ends the meeting, and the watchdog treated a missing badge as "unknown — never leave", so the VP kept recording video and holding a voice session until its maximum duration was reached. It now recognises Teams' post-meeting screen directly, and a badge that stays missing is bounded rather than ignored — while a brief disappearance (collapsed roster, content share) is still absorbed without ending a live meeting. Affects all launch types.

- **Speech is no longer transcribed twice.** Meeting audio was reaching the transcription sink by two separate routes, so Amazon Transcribe received every utterance twice and transcribed both — "Jack and Jill, Jack and Jill went up…". Human speech was affected on all platforms; the assistant's own speech was not, because it only ever had one route. There is now a single route, and a test asserts it stays that way.
- Added diagnostic logging for the Virtual Participant's audio path, visible in CloudWatch: the browser's requested and applied microphone settings, capture continuity (how much audio the browser actually captured per second of wall clock), the negotiated audio codec and bitrate, packet loss and jitter as reported by the far end, which captured track is transmitted and whether the browser's echo canceller is running on it, and which PulseAudio device each process is capturing from. This is groundwork for an unresolved report of crackly voice assistant audio on Microsoft Teams (the assistant sounds clean on Zoom) — that report remains open, and these measurements have so far ruled out several suspected causes rather than identified one.
- Reduced Virtual Participant CPU use: the avatar no longer redraws a full-resolution canvas on every frame when a meeting client requests an exact video size (Teams does), and the audio buffers were widened from 20ms to 80ms — still well below conversational latency.

- **Transcription and recording could go completely silent part-way through a meeting.** Meeting audio reached the transcription sink through a PulseAudio loopback, which does not keep a null sink out of the daemon's idle/suspend state — so the sink went digitally silent (a live Zoom session recorded 50 seconds at peak amplitude zero) while the audio it was supposed to be carrying was still flowing elsewhere. The application now holds an active playback stream on that sink instead, which both delivers the audio and keeps the sink running. Affects all launch types.

- **The meeting audio recording never contained the voice assistant's spoken replies.** The recording was written from the meeting-only audio source, which deliberately excludes the assistant so the voice assistant never hears itself — so the assistant's side of every conversation was missing from every audio recording, on every platform, while the video recording (taken from the combined source) had it. This went unnoticed because the assistant's transcript comes from the voice model's own text output rather than from Amazon Transcribe, so transcripts looked complete. The recording is now written from the same audio that is sent to Transcribe, so a recording is by construction what was transcribed.

- **MicroVM: the audio pipeline was being built twice.** The MicroVM lifecycle retries the image's readiness hook, which re-ran the container's audio setup, and PulseAudio silently renames a duplicate device rather than refusing it — so a running Virtual Participant had six audio sinks and eight sources where it should have had three and four. The harmful duplicate mixed the assistant's voice into the transcription sink twice at two different latencies. Because the readiness hook runs at image build time, the duplicate pipeline was captured in the snapshot and inherited by every launch. The setup is now idempotent, and the microphone device the meeting uses is pinned explicitly instead of being left to PulseAudio to choose.

- **Fixed meeting joins failing with a timeout after the page had already loaded.** The browser was given only 20 seconds to load a meeting page and was made to wait for every last analytics and advertising element on it, which on a busy page never finishes. Joins now allow a realistic amount of time, proceed as soon as the meeting page itself is ready, and no longer abandon a page that arrived slightly late. All platforms were affected; Zoom surfaced it first because it is the only one with a sign-in step before the join.

### Fixed

- **One bad client or call could kill the shared transcriber task and drop every concurrent meeting** — Three separate paths in the WebSocket transcriber took the whole Fargate task down with them: a Kinesis `put` was fire-and-forget, so a throughput exception became an unhandled rejection and process death; the message handler called `process.exit(1)` on a malformed frame or per-call I/O error; and there was no shutdown handling, so an ECS deploy, scale-in or health replacement `SIGKILL`ed the task mid-flight and lost end-of-call recording work silently. The Kinesis send is now awaited inside the surrounding try/catch, a per-socket error closes only that socket (with control-frame `JSON.parse` guarded separately), and a `SIGTERM`/`SIGINT` handler awaits in-flight end-of-call work with `StopTimeout: 120` so ECS allows the window.

- **Meeting recordings could silently overwrite each other** — The transcriber derived its S3 recording key from the call ID via a lossy filename sanitizer, so two differently titled meetings that normalized to the same string — or one meeting recorded twice — collided and the later recording replaced the earlier one with no error. Keys are now timestamped, matching the Virtual Participant's convention.

- **Transcription stopping or getting wrong timestamps during long meetings** — Amazon Transcribe streaming sessions end periodically on long calls, and both the WebSocket transcriber and the Virtual Participant scribe handled it incompletely: a graceful (non-error) server close ended transcription while audio kept flowing, and because Transcribe resets item `StartTime`/`EndTime` to 0 on each new request, post-reconnect segment and caption timestamps restarted from zero. Both now reconnect on clean closes as well as errors, carry a cumulative timeline offset so timestamps stay aligned with wall-clock across reconnects, count only *consecutive* failures toward the retry limit, and keep a meeting-long append-mode recording stream so a reconnect no longer truncates the recording. Hot-loop guards (a session must run ≥10s to count as healthy, plus a minimum backoff per reconnect) prevent a rapid close/reopen cycle from spinning, and the stale-session detector was tightened so an unrelated credential-expiry error no longer discards a still-valid `SessionId`. (#292, #465)

- **Teams Virtual Participant leaving meetings early on a transient roster misread** — The Teams VP could exit mid-conversation (recording stops hard, container exits with `EssentialContainerExited`/`exitCode=0`, VP row marked `COMPLETED`) whenever the Teams roster badge momentarily re-rendered, hid, or exposed an empty text node during a content-share transition, full-screen, or gallery/speaker re-layout — the old logic fired the graceful-leave path on the *first* reading that parsed to `<=1` attendees. Replaced with a debounced polling watchdog modeled on the one already proven on Zoom: it polls every 20s, requires **3 consecutive** genuine "`<=1` attendees" reads (~60s) before leaving, and treats a missing or empty badge as *unknown* rather than as "alone". (#317, #318, #399)

### Fixed

- **Virtual Participant stuck in `INITIALIZING` on ECS RunTask soft-failure** — When ECS RunTask returned HTTP 200 but with a non-empty `failures` array (typically the container instance agent disconnected briefly during scheduling), the Step Functions state machine treated it as a successful launch and the VP record sat at `INITIALIZING` indefinitely with no error feedback to the user. The state machine now inspects `runTaskResult.failures[]` explicitly via a new `CheckRunTaskFailures` Choice state and routes to a `MarkVPFailed` Pass + Lambda invoke that writes `FAILED` plus the original failure reason to the VP DynamoDB record. The detail page surfaces the `errorMessage` in the troubleshooting card. - **Virtual Participant silently broke when Zoom shipped UI changes** — Hard-coded CSS selectors in the Zoom platform handler (`#input-for-pwd`, `svg.SvgAudioMute`, `.video-avatar__avatar-footer span`, `.audio-level-indicator`, `.zm-modal`, etc.) would silently miss when Zoom updated its meeting client, causing the VP to hang or fail with cryptic browser-automation errors and requiring a code+container release to fix. - **VP host churn on newer ECS-optimized AMIs (May 2026+)** — The Amazon Linux 2 ECS-optimized AMI dropped its pre-installed `aws` CLI in mid-May 2026. The VP launch template's userdata had `aws ecr get-login-password ...` near the top with `set -e`, so on every new instance the script aborted at `aws: command not found` *before* `systemctl enable --now ecs` ran. The host registered with ECS via the auto-start service but failed its ECS health check minutes later, the ASG marked it unhealthy, and the cycle repeated every ~25 minutes — preventing any VP from running. Fixed by dropping `set -e`, adding a best-effort `yum install -y aws-cli` if the binary isn't present, and falling through to `systemctl enable --now ecs` regardless. The image-warm-pull behaviour is now best-effort (ECS still pulls the image at task launch via the task execution role).

- **ASG host-churn during stack updates** — The VP `AWS::AutoScaling::AutoScalingGroup` had no `UpdatePolicy`, so a CFN deploy that touched the launch template would drain the existing host while the new one was still booting + a stale ECS health check could mark the new instance unhealthy mid-rollout, leaving the cluster without a usable host for several minutes. Added `UpdatePolicy.AutoScalingRollingUpdate` with `SuspendProcesses: [HealthCheck, ReplaceUnhealthy, AZRebalance, AlarmNotification, ScheduledActions]` so health-check / replace / rebalance signals are paused during a rolling update. The capacity provider still launches new hosts on demand at runtime; this only affects how the ASG behaves during a stack update.

- **`list_meetings` MCP tool returned no rows for current-day meetings** — The DynamoDB GSI sort-key upper bound in `query_by_date_range` (`mcp_analytics/tools/list_meetings.py`) was built as `"ts#<end_iso>#~"`. Real SKs have the form `ts#<ISO8601>#id#<CallId>` (e.g. `ts#2026-05-27T17:34:…`), and `T` (0x54) sorts after `#` (0x23), so the `between(...)` query silently excluded every meeting whose SK had a `T` immediately after the date — which is *every* meeting whenever the caller didn't pass an explicit `end_date` past midnight (the default code path used a date-only end of `today.strftime("%Y-%m-%d")`). The upper bound is now `"ts#<end_iso>~"`; `~` (0x7E) sorts after every byte that can legally follow the date in the SK. Added a regression unit test (`tests/test_list_meetings_date_range.py`) that pins the exact `between` bounds via `botocore.stub.Stubber` so the bug can't silently return.

- **`make srt-clean` pre-scan cleanup** — New `scripts/srt/clean.py` + Make targets (`srt-clean`, `srt-clean-preview`, `srt-clean-checksums`) physically remove the build artifacts SRT's Bandit invocation can't be told to ignore (vendored Lambda-layer `python/` trees, `.aws-sam/`, `out/`, `node_modules/`, `dist/`, `build/`, `__pycache__/`, `.ash/`, intermediate scan JSONs). - **MCP API Gateway stage deployment failure in brand-new AWS accounts (complete fix)** — The initial fix removed only `MethodSettings.LoggingLevel: INFO` from `MCPApiKeyStage`, but the same account/region-wide `AWS::ApiGateway::Account.CloudWatchRoleArn` singleton is *also* required by REST API Gateway whenever `AccessLogSetting` is configured on a stage. In untouched accounts/regions the stage still failed to create with `CloudWatch Logs role ARN must be set in account settings to enable logging`. `AccessLogSetting` has now been removed from `MCPApiKeyStage` and the unused `MCPApiKeyAccessLogGroup` log group has been deleted, so the stage (and its parent nested stack `AISTACK`) now deploy cleanly into fresh accounts. Customers who want per-request MCP access logs can enable them manually after deployment: (1) create an IAM role trusted by `apigateway.amazonaws.com` with the `AmazonAPIGatewayPushToCloudWatchLogs` managed policy; (2) call `apigateway:UpdateAccount --cloud-watch-role-arn <role-arn>` once per region; (3) re-add `AccessLogSetting` on the MCP stage. CloudWatch request metrics on the stage (`MetricsEnabled: true`) are unchanged and continue to work without any account-level prerequisite.

- **`vp-loader` embed reliability and rendering** — Several fixes so the `component=vp-loader` iframe works reliably alongside the other VP embeds. The `onUpdateVirtualParticipant` AppSync subscription auto-resubscribes with capped exponential backoff after Amplify closes the WebSocket for token refresh; subscription merges no longer overlay `null` over previously-populated fields; `endVirtualParticipant` mutations include `endReason` and `endedBy`; `createVirtualParticipant` is followed immediately by a `getVirtualParticipant(id)` so the shared `VPConnectionDetails` render has all server-populated fields from the first paint; and the custom 2×2 "Virtual Participant Created" confirmation grid was replaced with the shared `StatusDetails` + `ConnectionDetails` components from `VirtualParticipantDetails.jsx`.

- **Web chat assistant failed to retrieve the transcript for meetings whose CallId contains `+`** — the chat iframe `src` interpolated `callId` into the query string without URL-encoding, and `URLSearchParams.get("callId")` then decoded the literal `+` as a space. Both chat iframe `src` assignments (`CallPanel.jsx` and `EmbedCallDetails.jsx`) now wrap the CallId with `encodeURIComponent`, restoring transcript retrieval for meeting IDs with `+` and any other URL-reserved characters.

- **Transcript / summary embeds showed "Error Loading Meeting" when loaded before the `callId` exists in DynamoDB** — `EmbedCallDetails.loadCall` returns `[null]` for not-yet-created calls, which then crashed inside `mapCallsAttributes` and hit the hard-error branch, masking the intended "Waiting for meeting to start..." state and blocking the AppSync `onCreateCall` auto-refresh. Null entries are now filtered before `mapCallsAttributes`, and "not found / does not exist / null / unauthorized" errors are classified as expected races so the subscription auto-load path takes over.

- **Slow bulk meeting delete from the UI** — the `meeting_controls_resolver` Lambda previously deleted meetings one at a time, and within each meeting deleted transcript segments and individual S3 objects serially — so deleting N meetings scaled roughly `N × (M_segments + S_s3_objects)` sequential round-trips. The resolver now (1) parallelises the per-meeting loop at `DELETE_MEETING_CONCURRENCY` (default 8), (2) parallelises transcript-segment deletes at `DELETE_SEGMENT_CONCURRENCY` (default 16) and runs them alongside VP cleanup, `getCall`, and S3 cleanup as four concurrent preparation steps, (3) replaces the per-object S3 `DeleteObject` loop with `DeleteObjects` batches of up to 1000 keys, and (4) expands the underlying urllib3 pool so concurrent AppSync calls don't serialise on the default 10-connection cap. Both concurrency dials are env-var tuneable. Bulk-delete of a 20-meeting selection goes from tens of seconds to a few seconds in typical runs.
