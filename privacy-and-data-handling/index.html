Privacy and Data Handling Controls

Privacy and Data Handling Controls

Section titled “Privacy and Data Handling Controls”

This page brings together, in one place, the controls that decide what LMA tells a meeting’s participants, what it captures and keeps, for how long, where the content is processed, and what is removed when a meeting or a whole deployment is deleted. It is written for an administrator or a reviewer deciding how to configure a deployment for internal meetings, customer-facing meetings or regulated meetings. Each section summarises a control and links to the page that describes it in full:

  • Meeting Consent — the consent modes, the consent record and how each capture method runs the consent flow.
  • Transcript Deletion After the Summary — deleting a meeting’s transcript once it is summarized.
  • Configuration Guide — the runtime settings, the Retention section and the configuration presets.
  • Infrastructure & Security — encryption, data retention and where meeting content is processed.
  • CloudFormation Parameters — the deploy-time parameters named below.

Two kinds of control appear throughout. Settings are stored in the deployment’s configuration and changed by an Admin on Configuration ▸ Configuration without a redeploy. Stack parameters are chosen when the CloudFormation stack is deployed or updated. Where a control is a stack parameter, this page says so, because no configuration preset or page can change it.

LMA records evidence of what participants were told and what they replied. It does not decide which recording, transcription or data-protection rules apply to a meeting, and nothing on this page is legal advice. Choose the configuration with whoever advises your organisation on those rules.

Consent options

Section titled “Consent options”

The three modes and the two opt-in rules

Section titled “The three modes and the two opt-in rules”

The consent mode is set for the whole deployment in the Meeting Consent section of the Configuration page and, where the deployment allows it, chosen again for a single meeting when it starts. It ships set to Off.

ModeWhat participants seeWhen the meeting is recorded and transcribed
OffNothingAlways. This is the shipped default.
NotifyA notice that the meeting is recorded and transcribed, and how to objectFrom the start, until a participant objects
Opt-inA request to agree, with the phrase to replyNot until the consent rule is met; an objection from anybody stops it again

An opt-in meeting is granted by one of two consent rules:

  • Each participant consents. Every participant listed as present has replied with consent. A participant who joins later and has not replied withholds recording and transcription again until they do.
  • The meeting owner attests that everyone agreed. The person who owns the meeting in LMA confirms that everyone agreed, out loud, in the chat or in the invitation’s terms. An objection from any participant still stops capture.

The other Meeting Consent settings decide the notice and request texts, the phrases a reply is read by, whether a meeting may be started with a different mode (Allow a different choice per meeting; with it off, a meeting may still choose a stricter mode or rule), whether evidence screenshots are kept, the model that checks them, what happens to streaming clients that show no consent step, whether a Virtual Participant leaves after an objection, and whether it reads replies said aloud. Their defaults are listed in The Meeting Consent settings. A saved change applies to meetings whose consent starts after the save.

How each capture method runs it

Section titled “How each capture method runs it”

The consent record and its rules are shared by every way a meeting reaches LMA; each capture method decides how the notice is shown and how replies are read (see Flows by capture method).

Virtual Participant. The Virtual Participant posts the notice in the meeting chat once it is admitted, and again when somebody joins. It reads replies typed in the chat by the sender’s display name and, with Read spoken replies on (the shipped default), replies said aloud and addressed to LMA (“LMA, I consent”, “LMA, I object”) under the name of the participant shown speaking. In an opt-in meeting it writes no transcript, recording, chat lines, key frames or screen recording until consent is given.

While consent is awaited and Read spoken replies is on, the Virtual Participant still sends the meeting audio to the meeting’s transcription engine, on a session of its own, only to hear spoken replies. What it hears is discarded inside the Virtual Participant: it does not reach the transcript, the recording, the summary or the logs, and only the reply events are kept on the consent record. With Amazon Transcribe that audio is processed by the Amazon Transcribe streaming service in the stack’s Region, and billed. Turn Read spoken replies off where no meeting audio may leave the Virtual Participant before consent; the Virtual Participant then reads the chat only, and an objection made out loud takes effect when the LMA user records it in LMA. No preset changes this setting. See Spoken replies.

What a Virtual Participant can read differs by meeting platform, which matters for the each-participant rule:

JoinEach participant consentsNotes
Chime, Zoom (browser and Meeting SDK), Google MeetSupportedThe platform shows chat senders and an attendee count. Google Meet’s sender and speaker reading has not been confirmed in a live meeting
Teams (browser)Supported aloud onlyTeams shows no chat sender, so a typed consent cannot be attributed. With Read spoken replies off the rule is never met
Teams (Azure Communication Services)SupportedThe platform gives the participants’ names
Webex (browser)Not supportedThe page shows no attendee count, so one unidentified participant is always listed. Use the owner-attests rule on Webex

Notify, owner-attests and objections work on every join. The full table, with how the participant list is screenshotted on each platform, is in What each platform supports.

Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App. Neither can see the meeting’s participants or chat, so the consent step is the LMA user’s own. They show the notice with a copy button for the user to share in the meeting, and ask the user to confirm that it was shared or, in opt-in, that everyone agreed (the owner-attests rule). The WebSocket transcriber records that confirmation on the meeting and decides whether audio is transcribed and recorded: in opt-in nothing is captured until the record allows it, and audio sent before then is discarded. Participant objected stops capture and ends the meeting. Because neither can collect each participant’s consent, an opt-in deployment whose rule is Each participant consents and that does not allow a different choice per meeting refuses Start from both. See Stream Audio and the Desktop Capture App.

Clients without a consent step. The browser extension, the Stream Audio embed, Desktop Capture App builds from before the consent step, and scripts written against the streaming API send no consent step. In Notify or Opt-in the setting Streaming clients without a consent step decides what happens to their meetings: Accept with a warning (the shipped default) captures them as before, with no consent record and a warning in the transcriber’s log, so in an opt-in deployment such a meeting is captured without anybody having confirmed consent; Refuse does not create, record or transcribe the meeting. Choose Refuse only once every client in use shows the consent step. See Clients without a consent step.

Upload Audio has no consent step: the recording already exists, and should only be uploaded with the consent its participants gave when it was made.

Objections

Section titled “Objections”

In every mode an objection, typed in the chat, said aloud to LMA, recorded by a signed-in user with Record an objection on the meeting page, or reported with Participant objected in Stream Audio or the Desktop Capture App, stops recording and transcription at once. What was captured before the objection is kept: an objection stops capture, it does not delete what was captured while capture was allowed. Delete the meeting if that content must go too.

With Virtual Participant leaves after an objection off (the default) the Virtual Participant stays without recording, and resumes if the objector later consents; the screen recording does not resume. With it on, the Virtual Participant leaves the meeting. See Objections.

Evidence kept

Section titled “Evidence kept”

What participants were told and replied is kept on the meeting as its consent record, an attribute of the meeting’s row, so it is shared, kept and deleted with the meeting. It holds the mode and rule, the status and whether capture was allowed, the notice text and each time it was posted, the participants listed as present, every reply and attestation with the participant’s display name, where it was read and when, the status history with reasons, and the evidence images with the result of their vision-model check. See What is stored.

Evidence images are screenshots of the meeting chat and participant list taken by the Virtual Participant when consent is granted and on every objection, or one frame captured by the user in Stream Audio or the Desktop Capture App. An Amazon Bedrock vision model can check each image (the Evidence check model, Amazon Nova Lite by default); its verdict is supporting evidence and never changes the meeting’s consent status. With Keep evidence screenshots off, an image is deleted once its check is stored. The consent record is included in the summary’s downloads.

Display names are not verified identities, and a model’s reading of a screenshot can be wrong; see Limitations.

Choosing a mode

Section titled “Choosing a mode”

The following describes how the modes fit common situations. It is not a statement of what any law requires.

  • Off suits deployments where participants are told about recording by other means, such as an organisation-wide policy or the meeting platform’s own notice, and for testing.
  • Notify suits internal meetings where a notice and the chance to object are what is expected. It works on every capture method and every Virtual Participant join. The Internal meetings – notify participants preset sets it.
  • Opt-in with each participant consenting suits meetings in which nothing may be captured until every participant has agreed. Only a Virtual Participant can collect it, and not on the Webex join; on the Teams browser join consent must be said aloud. The Regulated – consent required, transcript not retained preset sets it.
  • Opt-in with the owner attesting suits meetings whose consent is gathered outside LMA (in the invitation’s terms, or out loud at the start), meetings streamed from Stream Audio or the Desktop Capture App, and Virtual Participant joins that cannot list the participants. The record then rests on the owner’s attestation, plus any evidence image.

Whatever the mode, set Streaming clients without a consent step to Refuse in a deployment where every meeting must go through the consent step, and check first that no one relies on the browser extension or the Stream Audio embed.

A value saved in the Retention section applies to records written about five minutes after the save or later; a record already written keeps its expiry. The S3 lifecycle rules and the Virtual Participant records’ expiry follow the stack parameters only, so shortening a retention on the Configuration page does not remove files from the recordings bucket, or Virtual Participant records, sooner.

DynamoDB removes an expired item in the background, typically within a few days of its expiry rather than at it. The S3 lifecycle rules count whole days from when an object was written. The recordings bucket is versioned: when a lifecycle rule expires a file, Amazon S3 keeps its last version as a noncurrent version, and a further rule removes every noncurrent version one day after it stopped being current (see Earlier versions in the recordings bucket). A file is therefore gone from the bucket within about two days of the end of its retention period, rather than at it.

Earlier versions in the recordings bucket

Section titled “Earlier versions in the recordings bucket”

The recordings bucket has S3 Versioning turned on. When a lifecycle rule expires a file, Amazon S3 does not erase it: it adds a delete marker, and the file’s last version becomes a noncurrent version. An overwritten file keeps its earlier version in the same way. The bucket’s ExpireNoncurrentVersions lifecycle rule removes every noncurrent version one day after it stopped being current, and removes a delete marker once no version is left behind it, for every object in the bucket. Amazon S3 applies lifecycle rules once a day and rounds to the next midnight UTC, so an expired file can take up to about two days to leave the bucket entirely. In that time it is not served to anyone, because it is no longer the current version, but it still exists in the bucket.

The window is one day, where the configuration bucket keeps its replaced objects for 35: the configuration bucket’s earlier objects are what a configuration table restored from point-in-time recovery refers to, while nothing in LMA restores an earlier version of a recording or transcript file, and for meeting content the retention period is the point.

Deleting a meeting, and Delete the transcript after the summary, do not wait for the rule: they delete every version and delete marker of the files they remove at once. LMA’s other deletions in the bucket remove the current version and leave the rest to the rule: a consent evidence image deleted once checked, with Keep evidence screenshots off; an uploaded file once it is processed; and a Virtual Participant’s video segments once its video is assembled.

The knowledge base’s copy of the summary

Section titled “The knowledge base’s copy of the summary”

With the transcript knowledge base deployed, the summary step also writes the summary as a file in lma-transcripts/, next to the transcript file, so that meeting-history search can find a meeting by its summary. Both files are the knowledge base’s documents and follow the same rule: the lma-transcripts/ lifecycle rule, from TranscriptionExpirationInDays. The summary on the meeting record follows MeetingRecordExpirationInDays. A lifecycle rule takes whole days and the meeting record’s retention may be a fraction of a day, so the file cannot follow the meeting record’s parameter. With both parameters at their default of 90 days the two copies are kept equally long; to keep the file no longer than the meeting record, set TranscriptionExpirationInDays no higher than MeetingRecordExpirationInDays. Deleting the meeting deletes both copies.

Without the transcript knowledge base (TranscriptKnowledgeBase set to DISABLED), the summary file is not written, and the summary is kept on the meeting record only. Summary files written while a knowledge base was deployed stay until their lifecycle rule removes them.

Deleting data

Section titled “Deleting data”

Transcript deletion after the summary

Section titled “Transcript deletion after the summary”

Retention › Delete the transcript after the summary deletes each meeting’s transcript permanently once the meeting is summarized: Immediately, or 15, 30 or 60 minutes after the summary is stored. It ships set to Off, applies to the whole deployment, and an Admin can change it at any time; a change applies to the next meeting that ends. Unlike a retention period, it does not rely on DynamoDB’s background expiry: an EventBridge Scheduler schedule runs the deletion within the minute it is due.

  • When. The delay counts from when the summary is stored. If no summary is stored within 60 minutes of the meeting’s end, the delay counts from then, and the transcript is deleted whether or not a summary was written. A deletion made immediately is checked again ten minutes later, to remove anything written for the meeting after it. A scheduled deletion runs even if the setting has since been turned off.
  • What is deleted. Every transcript segment, the chat lines a Virtual Participant added, the meeting assistant answers, the transcript files in the recordings bucket (every version), and their entries in the transcript knowledge base.
  • What is kept. The summary (on the meeting and in the knowledge base), the meeting record, the audio and video recordings, key frames and their text, and the consent record. Turn Record meeting audio off and set EnableVideoRecording to false if the recordings must not be kept either.
  • What users see. The meeting page says when the transcript will be, or was, deleted; after it, Regenerate on the summary is refused.

As a backstop, while the setting is on, each transcript segment and assistant answer is written with a DynamoDB time to live of at most two days. A deletion that keeps failing is retried for up to 24 hours and then raises the transcript-deletion-dead-letter-queue-depth alarm (see Monitoring & Alerting). See Transcript Deletion After the Summary.

Deleting a meeting

Section titled “Deleting a meeting”

A meeting’s owner can delete it, and an Admin can delete any meeting, from the meetings list or from the meeting page’s ⋮ menu. People the meeting is shared with cannot delete it. The meeting disappears from their meeting lists, without a notification. See Meeting Deletion.

Deleting a meeting removes, for that meeting:

  • the meeting record and its meeting-list rows, and with them the summary, the agenda, the speaker names and the consent record;
  • every transcript item, partial or final, including chat lines and meeting assistant answers;
  • the usage records behind its cost estimate;
  • its key-frame items and images, and its consent evidence images;
  • its audio recording and its video recording, with any video segments still in the bucket;
  • the transcript and summary files in lma-transcripts/ whose names begin with the meeting’s ID; the transcript knowledge base drops their entries at its next sync;
  • every Virtual Participant record that names the meeting, after ending a Virtual Participant still in it.

Every file it removes from the recordings bucket is removed with all its versions and delete markers, so no earlier version is left behind. If a step fails, for example a file that Amazon S3 does not delete or a Virtual Participant record that cannot be removed, the meeting record is not deleted: the page reports that the deletion failed, the meeting stays in the list, and deleting it again repeats every step.

It does not change the copies listed in Copies that follow their own lifetime: the Kinesis and DynamoDB streams, point-in-time recovery, CloudWatch logs, and anything already downloaded or sent to an integration. In particular, the deleted meeting’s items can still be restored from the DynamoDB tables’ point-in-time recovery for 35 days. Deleting a user from User Management does not delete the meetings they own.

Deleting a stack

Section titled “Deleting a stack”

The EnableDataRetentionOnDelete stack parameter, true by default, decides what deleting the stack leaves behind:

  • true: the DynamoDB tables, the S3 buckets (recordings, logging, configuration history and Virtual Participant profiles), the transcript knowledge base’s vector bucket and the customer-managed KMS key are kept, with their contents.
  • false: the S3 buckets are emptied of every object version and deleted, and the vector bucket, the tables and the key are deleted with the stack (AWS KMS deletes a key only after its waiting period).

Most of the stack’s CloudWatch log groups are deleted with the stack, whatever the parameter says. The resources that do not follow these rules are listed in Retained Resources:

  • Kept whatever the parameter says: the voice enrollment table and the voice consent log (with EnableVoiceEnrollment=true); the log groups of the WebSocket transcriber’s and the Virtual Participant’s containers and of the scheduled Virtual Participant state machine; and the GraphQL API’s log group, which AppSync creates and the stack does not own. The log groups go on expiring their entries under their retention periods.
  • Kept only with true: the MicroVM log groups of the Virtual Participant and of the ASR MicroVM speech engine.
  • A deployment upgraded from a version before 0.4.0 may also have older log groups that the stack does not own.

See Cleanup for the steps to remove what remains.

Where content is processed

Section titled “Where content is processed”

Where meeting content is processed lists every place LMA sends meeting content. In summary:

  • The stack’s AWS Region. LMA’s own resources, the tables and buckets that store meeting content, and the services that carry it all run in the Region the stack is deployed to. CloudFront caches only the web UI’s static files.
  • Amazon Bedrock. The Model inference geography setting, in the Amazon Bedrock Models section, decides where every Bedrock model LMA calls may run: Global (the default; the shipped global. profiles may be served from any commercial Region), Same geography as this deployment, or In-region only. When the chosen geography offers no profile for a model, the feature reports an error rather than calling Bedrock outside it. The Nova Sonic voice assistant runs in the Region AmazonNovaSonicRegion names (this stack’s Region when blank), and knowledge-base embeddings run in the knowledge base’s Region. See Amazon Bedrock.
  • The transcription engine. Amazon Transcribe, in the stack’s Region, is the default for every meeting. Where the experimental on-demand engine is deployed (EnableMicrovmAsr=true), an Admin can move streaming meetings or Virtual Participants onto it in the Transcription Engine section; it runs on AWS Lambda MicroVMs in the stack’s Region, and the audio is not sent to Amazon Transcribe. A meeting whose MicroVM cannot start falls back to Amazon Transcribe. See Choosing the engine. Live translation on the meeting page calls Amazon Translate in the stack’s Region.
  • Redaction. Amazon Transcribe › Redact personal information (off by default) has Amazon Transcribe redact the entity types chosen in Entity types to redact from the transcript. It applies only to meetings transcribed by Amazon Transcribe in en-US, en-AU, en-GB or es-US; it does not apply to meetings using language identification or the on-demand engine, which has no redaction. It redacts the transcript text only: the audio recording, the video, key frames and their text, and the meeting chat are not redacted. See Content Redaction (PII).

Logging

Section titled “Logging”

The LogLevel stack parameter sets the Lambda functions’ and containers’ logging. At INFO (the default) and above, log lines record identifiers, sizes and outcomes rather than meeting content. At DEBUG they also record meeting content: the summary prompt with its transcript, meeting assistant requests and answers, and the voice assistants’ heard and spoken text. The AppSyncFieldLogLevel parameter sets what the GraphQL API logs: ERROR (the default) and NONE log no request content, while DEBUG and ALL log each request’s content, transcript segments included. Use either DEBUG setting only while diagnosing a problem. The audio a Virtual Participant hears while waiting for consent is not logged at any level.

Checklist for a regulated deployment

Section titled “Checklist for a regulated deployment”
  1. Apply Regulated – consent required, transcript not retained or Regulated – notify with opt-out, transcript not retained, at deploy time with ConfigurationPreset or from the Configuration page, and activate it.
  2. Decide Read spoken replies: off if no meeting audio may reach the transcription engine before consent.
  3. Under the consent-required preset, use Virtual Participants on joins that support the each-participant rule, and tell users that Stream Audio, the Desktop Capture App and the Webex join will not capture meetings.
  4. Set EnableVideoRecording to false, unless meeting video is wanted and its retention is acceptable.
  5. Keep LogLevel at INFO or WARN and AppSyncFieldLogLevel at ERROR or NONE.
  6. Set MeetingRecordExpirationInDays, TranscriptionExpirationInDays, AudioRecordingExpirationInDays and CloudWatchLogsExpirationInDays to the periods your policy allows, bearing in mind that evidence images and the knowledge base’s summary file follow TranscriptionExpirationInDays.
  7. Decide whether the transcript knowledge base (TranscriptKnowledgeBase) and any optional integration outside AWS may receive meeting content.
  8. Check that the chosen Model inference geography offers a profile for every model in use; the Amazon Bedrock Models section shows which features have none.
  9. Decide how the day or two for which the recordings bucket keeps noncurrent object versions, the 35-day DynamoDB point-in-time recovery window, and Amazon Bedrock model invocation logging in the account fit your policy.
  10. Set EnableDataRetentionOnDelete to match what must happen to the data when the deployment is removed.
